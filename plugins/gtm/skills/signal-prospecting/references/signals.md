# Signals in practice

## Why signals work

The biggest reported lever on cold reply rates is relevance: the right person, at the
right moment, about the right thing. Vendor figures (as of June 2026, from companies that
sell signal data, so read them as best case):

| Approach | Reported reply rate |
| --- | --- |
| Generic cold email | about 3 to 4% |
| Firmographic personalisation (industry, size, role) | about 5 to 8% |
| Anchored to a recent, real signal | about 15 to 30% when done well |

The exact numbers are marketing. The mechanism is sound: a message that is clearly about
something real and current at the reader's company is hard to dismiss as spam.

## Speed

Relevance decays quickly. Reaching out within 24 to 48 hours of a signal reportedly
multiplies response several times over. A week late, it is barely a signal. Thirty days
is a reasonable working limit; past 90 days, find a fresher reason.

## Choosing signals for a market

Work backwards from the problem the offer solves:

1. Write down the problem in one sentence.
2. Ask what event makes that problem suddenly worse, more visible, or newly owned by
   someone.
3. Ask where that event leaves a public trace.

Examples:

| Offer solves | Event that triggers it | Public trace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Messy month-end close | First finance hire, or a funding round bringing investor reporting | Job post for a finance role; funding announcement |
| Slow customer support | Support headcount growth, new region | Support job posts in a new location |
| Weak recruiting process | Hiring surge | Many open roles on the careers page at once |
| Compliance burden | Entering a regulated market or a new country | Launch announcement; local job posts |
| Event or venue logistics | Announcing a conference or a series of events | Event pages, ticketing pages, press |

## Search patterns

Adapt these to whatever search tool is available. Always open the primary source and
record its date.

- **Funding:** `"<industry>" "raises" OR "announces" "Series A" <month year>`;
  `site:<business-news-site> <industry> funding <region>`.
- **Hiring:** `"<role title>" "<industry>" jobs <city or country>`; the company's own
  `/careers` or `/jobs` page.
- **Launches:** `"<industry>" "launches" OR "introducing" <month year>`; the company's
  blog or changelog.
- **Leadership:** `"appoints" OR "joins as" "<role title>" "<industry>"`.
- **Tech stack:** job posts that name a specific tool ("experience with <tool>"); a
  vendor's public customer list; the company's engineering blog.
- **Buyer's own words:** `"<role title>" "<problem phrase>"` on public posts, talks and
  podcast transcripts.

## Judging signal quality

| Strong | Weak |
| --- | --- |
| Primary source (the company's own post or ad) | Aggregator summary with no link to the original |
| Dated within 30 days | Undated or older than 90 days |
| Names a problem the offer solves | Generic growth news with no link to the offer |
| Names a person or team you can contact | Only a company name |
| Two signals pointing the same way | One weak signal stretched to fit |

## What not to collect

- Personal details unrelated to the person's job.
- Anything from behind a login you are not entitled to use, or that a site's terms
  forbid collecting.
- Personal email addresses or phone numbers. Use business contact routes.
- Information about people who have opted out of contact.
