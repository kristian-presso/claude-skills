# Cadence and channels

As of June 2026. Touch counts and response figures here are reported by sales-tooling
vendors, who have a commercial reason to favour more touches and more channels. Treat
them as starting points and re-baseline against your own data.

## The shape most sources agree on

- **Touch count:** about 8 to 12 touches across all channels is the most-cited band.
  Email-only sequences are shorter, about 4 to 7 emails.
- **Persistence matters.** It takes roughly 8 touches on average to get a reply from a
  cold prospect, yet many senders stop after one or two. Closing that gap is the cheapest
  improvement available.
- **Duration:** about 17 to 27 days. Common framings are 8 to 12 touches over 2 to 4
  weeks, or 7 to 13 touches over 21 to 27 days.
- **Front-load week one.** A typical split is 4 to 5 touches in days 1 to 7, then 5 to 7
  more across days 8 to 21, with gaps widening as the window closes.

## When to stop

Replies arrive early and tail off fast.

- One follow-up model with touches on days 0, 3, 10 and 17 reports about 93% of all
  replies landing by day 10.
- For email, the first message reportedly captures a large share of replies (one source
  puts it near 58%), with follow-ups recovering the rest.
- Touch 13 and beyond rarely pays. Stop, park the contact, and come back later with a new
  reason rather than wearing out the channel and your sending reputation.

Two failure modes bracket the design: quitting too early, and grinding on after the reply
curve has gone flat.

## Channel mix

Single-channel outreach consistently does worse than multi-channel. One vendor claims
adding phone and social to email lifts response by almost three times; the exact figure
is marketing, but the direction is consistent across sources.

The point is not more volume. It is reaching people on the channel they actually attend
to, and building familiarity. A name seen on a professional network before the email
arrives lands better than a cold name in the inbox.

Rough weights across a sequence's touches (not a rule):

| Channel | Share of touches | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Email | 40 to 50% | The base. Carries the deliverability burden. |
| Phone | 20 to 30% | Deepens the contact. A voicemail counts as a touch. |
| Professional social | 15 to 25% | Recognition. A connection or a relevant comment warms the next email. |
| Video | 5 to 10% | An occasional differentiator, used sparingly. |

## Phasing

1. Open on **email**: lowest friction, on the prospect's terms.
2. Follow with a **call** and voicemail 2 to 3 days later.
3. Layer **social** touches throughout for credibility and a softer touch.

## A day-by-day template (10 touches, 21 days)

| Day | Channel | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Email | Signal-led opener, one soft ask |
| 1 | Social | Connection request, no pitch |
| 3 | Phone | Call; leave a 20-second voicemail if no answer |
| 3 | Email | Short note referencing the voicemail |
| 6 | Email | New angle: a specific outcome or piece of proof |
| 8 | Social | Comment on or react to something they posted, if genuine |
| 10 | Phone | Second call attempt |
| 13 | Email | A useful resource with no ask, or a simpler ask |
| 17 | Email | Different stakeholder angle, or "am I asking the wrong person?" |
| 21 | Email | Polite close: "I'll stop here; reply if the timing changes" |

Drop any channel the user will not actually run, and shorten rather than pad.

## Signals override the calendar

When a fresh trigger fires (a new hire, a funding announcement), reach out within 24 to
48 hours rather than waiting for the next scheduled step. Relevance decays by the day.
