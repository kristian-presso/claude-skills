# Value hypotheses

A value hypothesis is a short, falsifiable guess about what an account needs, why now, and
what it would be worth to them. Every first message carries one. Write two or three per
account and expect the buyer to correct them.

## The shape

```text
Because <sourced fact about the account, dated>,
<role> is likely trying to <outcome>.
<Product capability> could help by <specific mechanism>,
which would show up as <the metric or result they would notice>.
```

## Build them

1. Take the account's strongest signals and its committee map.
2. For each signal, ask what outcome it puts pressure on, and who owns that outcome.
3. Match the outcome to a capability the seller confirms the product has. If none fits,
   drop the hypothesis.
4. Name how they would notice the result, in their likely metric, not the vendor's.
5. Rank by strength of evidence and keep the top two or three.

For proposed use cases, use the same shape and add a first small step and what would make
it a bad fit.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Invented results.** No made-up percentages, savings or customer names.
- **Claiming knowledge of internal plans.** "You are rolling out X" is a guess presented
  as fact; "the job post suggests X matters" is honest.
- **A capability the product lacks.** The first technical call exposes it.
- **Five of them.** More than three dilutes the one that is right.

## Check

Each hypothesis names a source, a role, a capability the seller confirmed, and a result
the buyer would notice. Read it as the buyer: if they could not reply "yes, that's us" or
"no, it's actually Y", it is not specific enough.
