# Motion types

There are three motions and they need different machinery. Pick one primary motion per
seller before building anything else.

| Question | New-logo outbound | Named territory | Expansion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who chose the accounts? | the seller, from an ICP | the employer, as a list | already customers |
| Typical seller | founder, SDR, small-business owner | enterprise or strategic AE | account manager, CSM, AE with a book |
| Account count | hundreds | 10 to 80 | 5 to 100 |
| Main constraint | finding fit and timing | reaching the right people | finding the next use case and its owner |
| Signal source | public: news, hiring, funding, tech | public, plus the seller's own notes | product usage, renewals, support, org changes |
| Effort per account | minutes | hours | hours |

## What follows from the choice

- **Tiers.** Outbound tiers a long list; a territory tiers a short one.
- **Plays.** Outbound uses one light play per tier. A territory and expansion get a
  bespoke play for each tier-1 account.
- **Cadence.** Outbound sweeps the market weekly. A territory sweeps the named list weekly
  and reviews tier-1 accounts in depth monthly.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Outbound tactics on a named territory.** Spraying a sequence at every contact in a
  30-account patch burns accounts the seller cannot replace.
- **Treating expansion as new business.** A customer who gets a cold prospecting email
  from their own vendor reads it as the vendor not knowing them.
- **An implied motion.** If the seller cannot say which one they run, the plan mixes all
  three and does none.

## Check

The choice is right when the seller agrees with who chose the accounts, how many there
are, and where the signals come from. Revisit it if after four weeks most effort is going
on accounts outside the motion.
