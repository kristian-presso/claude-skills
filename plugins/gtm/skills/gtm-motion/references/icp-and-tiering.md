# ICP and tiering

An ideal customer profile (ICP) is the set of observable traits shared by accounts that
buy, buy fast and stay. Write it from the seller's best customers, not from who they wish
bought. Score **fit** separately from **timing**, then tier on both.

## Write the ICP

1. Take three to five best customers or wins. With none, use the product's clearest use
   case and label the ICP a hypothesis.
2. For each, record traits someone outside could check: industry, size band, region,
   business model, tech in use, the team that buys, regulatory pressure.
3. Keep the traits they share. List **disqualifiers** - traits that predict a lost or bad
   deal - separately.
4. Write it as one paragraph a stranger could use to say yes or no in a minute.

## Score fit

Score each trait 0 to 2 (no match, partial, strong), double the two or three that matter
most, and scale to a score out of 10. Any disqualifier caps fit at 2.

## Tier on fit and timing

Timing comes from the signal score (see `signal-scoring.md`).

| | Timing high | Timing low |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fit high** | Tier 1 - bespoke play this week | Tier 2 - watch weekly, occasional relevant touch |
| **Fit low** | Tier 3 - one light touch, only if cheap | Out - do not work |

Keep tier 1 to ten accounts or fewer. Accounts move between tiers each sweep as timing
changes; fit changes rarely.

## Buying roles

Complex purchases are decided by a group - commonly reported as six to ten people.

| Role | Cares about |
| --- | --- |
| Economic buyer | outcome, cost, risk to their targets |
| Champion | their own problem, looking good internally |
| User lead | daily workflow, effort saved |
| Technical evaluator | fit with the stack, security, effort to adopt |
| Procurement and legal | terms, compliance, vendor risk |
| Blocker | the status quo, a rival tool, their own project |

## Mistakes to avoid

- **An ICP written from the whole market.** "Mid-market and enterprise" scores every
  account the same.
- **Traits nobody can observe.** "Innovative culture" becomes whatever the scorer feels.
- **One merged score.** A strong-fit account with nothing happening and a poor fit with a
  funding round score the same, and both get the wrong treatment.
- **A tier 1 of 40.** If everything is tier 1, the tiering has failed.

## Check

Score the seller's past wins and losses. Wins should land at fit 7 or above and losses
clearly lower. If they do not separate, the traits are wrong.
