## Annual accounts

Every company files accounts at Companies House every year, even if dormant. They also go to shareholders and to HMRC with the Company Tax Return.

| Accounts | Deadline for a private company |
|---|---|
| Normal year | 9 months after the accounting reference date (year end) |
| First accounts covering more than 12 months | 21 months after incorporation, or 3 months after the accounting reference date, whichever is longer |
| After shortening the year end | 9 months from the new date, or 3 months from the AA01 notice, whichever is longer |

Your first year end is the last day of the month of your incorporation anniversary (incorporated 06/04/2025, year end 30/04/2026). A period can be extended to at most 18 months.

### Size thresholds (changed for periods starting on or after 06/04/2025)

A company qualifies if it meets at least 2 of the 3 tests.

| Size | Turnover | Balance sheet total | Average employees | Old limits (periods before 06/04/2025) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Micro-entity | £1 million or less | £500,000 or less | 10 or fewer | £632,000 / £316,000 / 10 |
| Small | £15 million or less | £7.5 million or less | 50 or fewer | £10.2 million / £5.1 million / 50 |

### What size lets you file today

- **Micro-entity**: simpler accounts; file only a balance sheet with less information; audit exemption; no directors' report needed.
- **Small**: audit exemption; you can choose not to file the profit and loss account or directors' report (state this on the balance sheet); abridged accounts if all members agree.
- **Dormant**: no profit and loss account or directors' report needed at Companies House.

### Changes coming from 01/04/2028

Confirmed by government on 09/06/2026 (delayed from April 2027):

- **Software-only filing**: all accounts filed in iXBRL through commercial software. WebFiling and paper close for accounts (WebFiling stays for other filings).
- Small and micro companies must file a profit and loss account, with an option to opt out of publishing it.
- Abridged accounts are abolished.
- A stronger audit-exemption statement, and a business reason to shorten the year end more than once in 5 years.

HMRC already requires software: its free accounts and tax return service closed on 31/03/2026.

### Late filing penalties (private company)

| How late | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Up to 1 month | £150 |
| 1 to 3 months | £375 |
| 3 to 6 months | £750 |
| More than 6 months | £1,500 |

The penalty doubles if accounts are late 2 years in a row. Late filing is also a criminal offence for directors, and the company can be struck off. Appeals blaming first accounts, your accountant or not knowing the deadline may not succeed.

## Confirmation statement

- File at least once every 12 months. The review period ends 12 months after incorporation (first statement) or after the last confirmation statement date. You have **14 days** after the review period ends to file.
- You must file even if nothing has changed, and confirm the company's intended future activities are lawful.
- **Fee: £50 online, £110 on paper** (form CS01), payable once per 12-month payment period.
- Failing to file can mean a fine of up to £5,000 and strike-off.
- Companies House will not accept it until **every director has verified their identity** and supplied their personal code.

## Registered email address

Since 04/03/2024 every company must have a registered email address (add it in your confirmation statement if missing). It is not published; Companies House uses it for official contact, so monitor it. Since the same date the registered office must be an "appropriate address", not a PO Box.

## Identity verification (ECCTA)

- **From 18/11/2025** identity verification is a legal requirement. New directors and people with significant control (PSCs) must verify at incorporation or appointment.
- **Existing directors** supply their Companies House personal code in the company's next confirmation statement during a 12-month transition that started on 18/11/2025.
- **PSCs** have their own 14-day window to provide their code; the dates depend on whether they were registered before 18/11/2025 and whether they are also a director.
- Verify free through GOV.UK One Login (app, online questions or a Post Office), or via an Authorised Corporate Service Provider such as your accountant. You usually verify once.
- Acting as a director past your deadline without verifying is an offence for you and possibly the company and other directors.

## Statutory registers and records

Since 18/11/2025, the Companies Act sections requiring companies to keep their own register of directors, register of directors' residential addresses and register of PSCs have been removed; that information is held at Companies House. You must still keep, and make available for inspection at the registered office or one alternative inspection address:

- register of members (shareholders), including historic entries
- directors' service contracts and indemnities
- resolutions and minutes of general meetings
- register of charges and charge documents, where relevant

Keep proper accounting records as well.

**PSCs** are people who own or control the company, for example by holding more than 25% of shares or voting rights. Register them with Companies House and report any change within 14 days of confirming it. A sole founder is usually their own PSC.

## Directors' duties in plain English

The Companies Act 2006 sets seven general duties:

1. Act within the company's constitution and use powers only for their proper purpose.
2. Promote the success of the company for its members as a whole, considering employees, customers, suppliers, the community and long-term consequences.
3. Use your own independent judgement.
4. Take reasonable care, skill and diligence.
5. Avoid conflicts of interest.
6. Do not accept benefits from third parties because you are a director.
7. Declare any personal interest in a proposed company transaction.

In certain circumstances, such as financial difficulty, other rules require directors to consider creditors' interests. Directors stay responsible for records, accounts and filings even when an accountant prepares them.

## Director's loan accounts

Money you take out that is not salary, dividends or a repaid expense is a director's loan. Keep records.

- If the loan is still unpaid **9 months after the company's year end**, the company pays a temporary tax under **section 455** at a rate tied by law to the dividend upper rate for the tax year the loan was made: **35.75% for loans made from 06/04/2026** (33.75% for 2022-23 to 2025-26 loans; 32.5% before 06/04/2022). GOV.UK's director's loans page still shows 33.75%.
- The charge is refunded 9 months and 1 day after the end of the period in which the loan is repaid or written off; interest is not refunded.
- Anti-avoidance rules catch repaying a loan over £5,000 and re-borrowing within 30 days, or arranging new loans over £15,000.
- Loans over £10,000 at any time in the year are a benefit in kind, reported on the director's Self Assessment.

See [paying yourself as a director](paying-yourself-as-a-director.md).

## Closing a company

If the company has not traded, sold stock or changed name in the last 3 months, is not threatened with liquidation and has no agreement with creditors (such as a CVA), directors can apply for voluntary strike-off on form DS01 (**£13 online, £18 on paper**). First close down properly: tell HMRC, deal with staff, file final accounts and a Company Tax Return with HMRC, pay all tax and share out the assets. Within 7 days, send a copy of the application to shareholders, creditors, employees, pension fund managers and any non-signing director. If nobody objects, the company is struck off after the 2-month notice period. Assets taken out may be taxed as a capital gain, but amounts over £25,000 are treated as income; anything left, including later HMRC refunds, goes to the Crown. Otherwise you need a voluntary liquidation.
