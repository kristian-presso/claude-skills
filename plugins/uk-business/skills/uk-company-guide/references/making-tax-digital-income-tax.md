# Making Tax Digital for Income Tax

> Reference, not legal, tax or accounting advice. Figures researched against the official sources listed at the end on 06/10/2026; key figures re-checked on gov.uk on 16/10/2026. Confidence: high. Re-check by 06/01/2027, or sooner after a Budget or Spring Statement.

This page is for sole traders and landlords in the UK who file Self Assessment. Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax started on 06/04/2026. It changes how you keep records and report to HMRC: digital records, a short update every quarter, and an annual tax return sent from software. It does not change how much tax you pay or when you pay it. This is reference, not advice. Limited companies are not in MTD for Income Tax; it is about your personal self-employment and property income.

## Who is in scope and when

HMRC looks at your **qualifying income** on the Self Assessment return for an earlier tax year and tells you if you must join.

| Qualifying income | Measured on the tax return for | You must use MTD from |
|---|---|---|
| Over £50,000 | 2024-25 | 06/04/2026 |
| Over £30,000 | 2025-26 | 06/04/2027 |
| Over £20,000 | 2026-27 | 06/04/2028 |

HMRC checks your qualifying income every year from your return. Anyone below the threshold can volunteer. Partnerships do not currently need to use MTD for Income Tax; HMRC says they will in the future, with timing to be confirmed.

Practical reading: if your 2025-26 return (due 31/01/2027) shows self-employment plus property turnover over £30,000, expect to be in MTD from 06/04/2027.

## What qualifying income means

Qualifying income is your total **gross** income (turnover, before expenses) from:

- self-employment
- property, UK and foreign (if you are UK tax resident), including your share of jointly owned property

It does **not** include employment income through PAYE, dividends (including from your own company), your share of partnership profits, or state and private pensions.

GOV.UK example: rental income of £25,000 plus self-employment income of £27,000 gives qualifying income of £52,000, which is over the £50,000 threshold, even though neither source alone is.

This matters for founders: a company director paid by salary and dividends is not brought in by that pay, but a side-hustle sole trade or rental income is.

## What you have to do

1. **Keep digital records** of every self-employment and property income and expense transaction, in compatible software.
2. **Send a quarterly update** for each business (summaries of your records) four times a year.
3. **Submit your tax return** (the final declaration) through software by 31 January after the tax year, adding any other income and making year-end adjustments.
4. **Pay** as normal under Self Assessment: 31 January and 31 July, including payments on account. See [self assessment](self-assessment.md).

## Quarterly update deadlines

Each update is cumulative: it covers the start of the tax year to the end of the quarter. You can use standard quarters or opt for calendar quarters. The deadlines are the same.

| Update | Standard period | Calendar period | Deadline | 2026-27 date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 6 Apr to 5 Jul | 1 Apr to 30 Jun | 7 August | 07/08/2026 |
| Q2 | 6 Apr to 5 Oct | 1 Apr to 30 Sep | 7 November | 07/11/2026 |
| Q3 | 6 Apr to 5 Jan | 1 Apr to 31 Dec | 7 February | 07/02/2027 |
| Q4 | 6 Apr to 5 Apr | 1 Apr to 31 Mar | 7 May | 07/05/2027 |
| Tax return (final declaration) for 2026-27 | | | 31 January | 31/01/2028 |

Your 2025-26 return, due 31/01/2027, is still filed the normal way: MTD covers 2026-27 onwards for the first group.

## Signing up

- You need to be registered for Self Assessment, have filed a return in the last 2 years, and have compatible software.
- Sign up on GOV.UK with your Self Assessment user ID and password. Have your business start date, business name, address and trade description ready.
- From September 2026 HMRC said it would sign up eligible people automatically if they have not done it themselves. Signing up yourself is better because your details are checked at the start.

## Software

Compatible software must let you create, store and correct digital records, send quarterly updates and submit your tax return. Two routes:

- **Full software:** bookkeeping app that records transactions (often with bank feeds or receipt scanning) and files directly.
- **Bridging software:** links to records you keep in spreadsheets or another tool and sends the updates and return.

Some free products exist for simple affairs, usually with transaction limits. Use HMRC's software finder on GOV.UK to filter by your income types.

## Exemptions

**Automatically exempt (no need to apply)** include:

- partnerships (for now)
- people with qualifying income of £20,000 or less
- people without a National Insurance number before the tax year starts
- non-resident companies, trusts and personal representatives
- Lloyd's members
- people not physically or mentally capable of dealing with HMRC who have a power of attorney or legal guardian
- ministers of religion using the SA102M page
- people claiming Married Couple's Allowance or Blind Person's Allowance

**Must apply: digitally excluded.** You can apply if it is not reasonable for you to use software because of age, health condition or disability, because your religious beliefs are incompatible with digital communications, or because you cannot get internet access at home or at your business due to location. HMRC will not accept: you filed on paper before, you are not familiar with software, you have few records, or it costs time or money.

**How to apply:** phone or write to HMRC using the Self Assessment general enquiries contact details. An agent, or a friend or family member with your authority, can apply for you. HMRC aims to reply within 28 calendar days.

## Penalties

If you are in MTD, new penalties replace the old Self Assessment late filing and late payment penalties.

### Late submission: points

- You get 1 penalty point for each missed deadline (only one point per deadline).
- At 4 points you get a £200 penalty, and another £200 for each further missed deadline.
- **Soft landing:** for 2026-27 there are no penalty points for late quarterly updates. Points apply to quarterly updates for tax years after 2026-27. The tax return deadline still counts in 2026-27.
- Points below 4 expire 24 months after the missed deadline. Once at 4 points, you must file everything on time for 12 months and clear outstanding submissions from the previous 24 months to reset.

### Late payment

| Days late | 2026-27 | 2027-28 |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 15 | No penalty | No penalty |
| 16 to 30 | 3% of tax owed at day 15 (none in your first year) | 4% of tax owed at day 15 (none in your first year) |
| 31 or more | 3% at day 15 plus 3% at day 30, plus 10% a year charged daily from day 31 for up to 2 years | 4% at day 15 plus 4% at day 30, plus 10% a year charged daily from day 31 for up to 2 years |

In your first year of the new penalties you have 30 days from the due date to pay in full or set up a payment plan; after that it is 15 days. Late payment interest is charged on top.

## What to do now

1. Check your last return: add up self-employment and property turnover.
2. If you are over £50,000 on 2024-25, you should already be in MTD. Check your Q1 update (07/08/2026) went in and diarise Q2 for 07/11/2026.
3. If you are over £30,000 on 2025-26, choose software before 06/04/2027 and move records into it.
4. Build the quarterly deadlines into [key deadlines calendar](key-deadlines-calendar.md).
5. If you are VAT registered, MTD for VAT is separate. See [vat schemes mtd and returns](vat-schemes-mtd-and-returns.md).
