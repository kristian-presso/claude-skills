# Corporation Tax for small UK companies

> Reference, not legal, tax or accounting advice. Figures researched against the official sources listed at the end on 06/10/2026; key figures re-checked on gov.uk on 16/10/2026. Confidence: high. Re-check by 06/01/2027, or sooner after a Budget or Spring Statement.

For founders running a UK limited company, including a side-hustle company with one director. It covers rates, registration, deadlines, penalties, what you can deduct and the main allowances. This is reference material, not tax advice: rules change at each Budget and your own figures matter, so check with an accountant before you rely on it. Sole traders do not pay Corporation Tax; see [self assessment](self-assessment.md) and [legal forms](legal-forms.md).

## The rates (financial year starting 01/04/2026)

Corporation Tax years run from 1 April. The rates have been the same since 01/04/2023 and are unchanged for the year starting 01/04/2026.

| Taxable profit (12-month period, no associated companies) | Rate |
|---|---|
| £50,000 or less | 19% small profits rate |
| £50,001 to £250,000 | 25% main rate, reduced by Marginal Relief |
| Over £250,000 | 25% main rate |

**Marginal Relief** uses a standard fraction of 3/200. In practice, relief is 3/200 x (£250,000 minus profit), so a company with £100,000 profit pays £25,000 minus £2,250 = £22,750. Each extra pound of profit between the two limits is effectively taxed at 26.5%, which matters when deciding how much to pay out as salary or pension. HMRC has a Marginal Relief calculator.

**The limits shrink** in two cases:

- **Short accounting periods**: the £50,000 and £250,000 limits are reduced in proportion (a 6-month period uses £25,000 and £125,000).
- **Associated companies**: divide both limits by the number of associated companies plus one. With 3 associated companies, the limits become £12,500 and £62,500. Companies are generally associated if one controls the other or the same person or people control both, wherever they are resident. A founder who owns two companies should check this first.

Dividends received from unrelated, unassociated companies count towards the £250,000 test ("augmented profits").

## Registering

- Most companies are set up for Corporation Tax when they incorporate at Companies House.
- If not, add Corporation Tax to your HMRC business tax account when you start to do business (buying, selling, advertising, renting a property or employing someone). You need the company number, the date you started business, the date your first accounts run to and the 10-digit UTR (request it online if it has not arrived within 15 working days).
- The legal deadline to notify HMRC is 3 months after your first accounting period begins (Finance Act 2004, section 55).
- A company that is not doing business is usually dormant for Corporation Tax. See [registration and identifiers](registration-and-identifiers.md).

## Accounting periods

An accounting period for Corporation Tax cannot be longer than 12 months. It normally matches your company's financial year, but often differs in the first year. If your first accounts cover more than 12 months, you file **two** Company Tax Returns: one for the first 12 months and one for the rest.

## Deadlines and payment

| What | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Pay Corporation Tax (profits up to £1.5 million) | 9 months and 1 day after the end of the accounting period |
| File the Company Tax Return (CT600, with accounts and computations) | 12 months after the end of the accounting period |

You do not get a bill. You must file a return even if you made a loss or owe nothing, and tell HMRC using the "nil to pay" form if nothing is due. Each accounting period has its own 17-character payment reference.

**Software is now required.** HMRC's free "File your accounts and Company Tax Return" service closed on 31/03/2026. From 01/04/2026 you file using commercial software (or an agent). Paper is only allowed with a reasonable excuse or for filing in Welsh.

## Quarterly instalments (large companies)

| Profits (annual rate) | How you pay |
|---|---|
| Up to £1.5 million | Single payment, 9 months and 1 day after the period ends |
| Over £1.5 million and under £20 million | 4 quarterly instalments |
| Over £20 million | Different, earlier instalment rules |

The £1.5 million (and £10 million) thresholds are divided by the number of associated companies plus one. For a 12-month period, instalments fall 6 months and 13 days after the period starts, then every 3 months; the final one is 3 months and 14 days after the period ends. A company is not caught if its total liability is under £10,000, or if profits are £10 million or less and it was not large in the previous 12 months (or did not exist then).

## Late filing penalties

| Time after the filing deadline | Penalty |
|---|---|
| 1 day | £200 |
| 3 months | Another £200 |
| 6 months | HMRC estimates the bill (a "tax determination") and adds 10% of unpaid tax |
| 12 months | Another 10% of unpaid tax |

If the return is late 3 times in a row, each £200 becomes £1,000. Interest is charged on late payments. Appeal online if you had a reasonable excuse, but you must file first.

## What you can and cannot deduct

A cost is deductible if it is a **revenue** expense (day-to-day running costs) incurred **wholly for a business purpose**. **Capital** spending (assets used long term) is not deducted directly but may qualify for capital allowances.

| Usually deductible | Not deductible |
|---|---|
| Accountancy and bookkeeping fees | Entertaining clients and business gifts (with limited exceptions) |
| Salaries, employer National Insurance | Dividends paid to shareholders |
| Employer pension contributions, if part of a remuneration package paid wholly for the business | Buying a van, server or land (claim capital allowances instead) |
| Software subscriptions, rent, insurance, advertising | Anything with a personal or non-business purpose |
| Staff entertainment within the employee exception | |

Anything a director or employee gets personal use from is a benefit and has its own tax rules (see [paying yourself as a director](paying-yourself-as-a-director.md)). Keep receipts and a note of the business reason for each cost.

## Capital allowances

| Allowance | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Annual Investment Allowance (AIA) | 100% deduction on qualifying plant and machinery up to **£1 million** a year (pro-rated for short periods). Not for cars. |
| Full expensing (companies only) | 100% deduction on new and unused main-rate plant and machinery bought from 01/04/2023, with no upper limit |
| 50% first-year allowance (companies only) | 50% on new special-rate assets bought from 01/04/2023 |
| 40% first-year allowance | 40% on new and unused main-rate assets bought on or after 01/01/2026 (not cars) |
| 100% first-year allowance | New zero-emission cars and EV charge points bought before April 2027 |
| Writing down allowances | Main pool **14%** from 01/04/2026 (18% before); special rate pool 6% |

Cars use writing down allowances based on CO2 emissions unless they qualify for the 100% allowance. Reduce any claim for private use.

## Losses in brief

A trading loss can be set against other profits of the same period, carried back against profits of the previous 12 months (claim within 2 years of the end of the loss period), or carried forward. For carried-forward losses, profits above a £5 million allowance can only be reduced by 50%, which rarely affects small companies.

## R&D relief

For accounting periods starting on or after 01/04/2024, the merged R&D expenditure credit (20%) and enhanced R&D intensive support for loss-making R&D-intensive SMEs replaced the old schemes. R&D relief has its own detailed rules; read HMRC's guidance (linked below) before claiming.

## What to do

1. Confirm Corporation Tax is set up and diarise your first accounting period dates.
2. Choose filing software (or an accountant) now that HMRC's free service has closed.
3. Put aside roughly 19% to 26.5% of profit as you go, depending on your profit level.
4. Diarise both deadlines in [key deadlines calendar](key-deadlines-calendar.md) and check [company annual compliance](company-annual-compliance.md) for the separate Companies House deadline.
