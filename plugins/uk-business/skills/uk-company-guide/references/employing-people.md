# Employing people in the UK

> Reference, not legal, tax or accounting advice. Figures researched against the official sources listed at the end on 06/10/2026; key figures re-checked on gov.uk on 16/10/2026. Confidence: medium. Re-check by 06/01/2027, or sooner after a Budget or Spring Statement.

For UK founders taking on help for the first time: a freelancer, a contractor or a first employee. This is reference material, not legal or HR advice. Employment law is changing in phases under the Employment Rights Act 2025, so check dates and talk to Acas (0300 123 1100) or an adviser for anything contentious.

## Freelancer, worker or employee?

Status decides what rights the person has and what you must do. Only a court or tribunal makes the final call, and someone can have one status for employment law and another for tax.

| Status | Likely if most of these are true | What you owe them |
|---|---|---|
| Self-employed / contractor | They quote for work, are not directly supervised, invoice you, pay their own tax and NI, get no holiday or sick pay | What the contract says, plus health and safety and (in some cases) discrimination protection |
| Worker | Casual, freelance or zero-hours style work; they must do it personally; you supervise them; you are not obliged to offer work and they are not obliged to accept | National Minimum Wage, paid holiday, rest breaks, 48-hour week (opt-out allowed), discrimination and whistleblowing protection |
| Employee | Must work regular minimum hours, managed by you, cannot send a substitute, gets paid holiday and sick pay, uses your premises and equipment | Everything a worker gets plus statutory sick pay, family leave and pay, notice, redundancy pay, unfair dismissal protection, flexible working requests |

For tax, use HMRC's Check Employment Status for Tax (CEST) tool and keep the result. Getting it wrong can mean unpaid tax and penalties.

## IR35 (off-payroll working) in brief

The rules apply, contract by contract, when a contractor works through their own limited company but would be an employee if engaged directly.

- **Small private-sector client:** the contractor's own company decides whether IR35 applies and handles the tax. You only have to confirm your size if asked.
- **Medium or large private-sector client, or any public body:** you must decide the status, issue a status determination statement (SDS) with reasons, and if "inside", the fee payer deducts tax and NI.
- HMRC's client guidance (last updated 30/08/2024) treats you as medium or large if you meet 2 or more of: turnover over £10.2 million, balance sheet over £5.1 million, more than 50 employees. Confirm the current test with your accountant, as company size rules have moved since. See [legal forms](legal-forms.md).

## Hiring your first employee: step by step

1. **Check the right to work before day one.** Use the Home Office online service with the applicant's share code, check original documents with the person present, or use a certified identity service provider. British and Irish citizens cannot get a share code, so check their passport or use a provider. Keep a copy you cannot alter for the whole employment plus 2 years after it ends, and record the date. Skipping this risks a civil penalty if they turn out not to have the right to work.
2. **Give a written statement of particulars on day one.** The principal statement (pay, hours, holiday, place of work, start date, job title, probation, notice and more) is due on the first day. The wider statement (for example pensions and training) is due within 2 months. Tell staff about any change within one month.
3. **Register as an employer with HMRC** before the first payday, but not more than 2 months before you start paying people. You must register even if the only person you pay is yourself as sole director. Your employer PAYE reference arrives by letter. See [paying yourself as a director](paying-yourself-as-a-director.md).
4. **Run payroll under RTI (Real Time Information).** On or before each payday send a Full Payment Submission (FPS) through payroll software, give payslips, and deduct tax and NI. Pay HMRC by the 22nd of the next tax month (19th by post). Send an Employer Payment Summary (EPS) by the 19th to reclaim statutory pay or if you paid nobody that month. HMRC closes a new scheme if nothing is reported or paid in 120 days. NI rates sit in [personal tax and national insurance](personal-tax-and-national-insurance.md).
5. **Get employers' liability insurance** from day one: at least £5 million cover from an authorised insurer, certificate displayed where staff can see it (a website or intranet counts). Fines are up to £2,500 for every day uninsured and £1,000 for not displaying or showing the certificate. Exempt if you only employ close family members.
6. **Set up a workplace pension** (see below) and diarise the duties in [key deadlines calendar](key-deadlines-calendar.md).

## National Minimum Wage and Living Wage from April 2026

| Age or status | Hourly rate from April 2026 | April 2025 to March 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| 21 and over (National Living Wage) | £12.71 | £12.21 |
| 18 to 20 | £10.85 | £10.00 |
| Under 18 | £8.00 | £7.55 |
| Apprentice | £8.00 | £7.55 |

The apprentice rate applies to apprentices under 19, or 19 and over in the first year of their apprenticeship.

## Holiday

Almost all workers get 5.6 weeks' paid holiday a year. Five days a week means at least 28 days, and that is the statutory cap even for 6-day weeks. Bank holidays can count towards it. Part-timers get it pro rata (3 days a week = 16.8 days). Irregular-hours and part-year workers accrue leave by hours worked.

## Statutory Sick Pay

- From 06/04/2026 the Employment Rights Act removed the Lower Earnings Limit and the waiting days.
- Rate: £123.25 a week or 80% of average weekly earnings (usually averaged over 8 weeks), whichever is lower.
- Paid for qualifying days (days they normally work) from the first full day off sick, for up to 28 weeks, through payroll.
- You can only ask for a fit note after more than 7 days off in a row.

## Workplace pension (auto-enrolment)

- Duties start the day your first employee starts work (your "duties start date"). Postponement of up to 3 months is allowed if you write to staff.
- Enrol everyone aged 22 to State Pension age earning over £10,000 a year (£833 a month, £192 a week) who normally works in the UK.
- Minimum contributions on qualifying earnings (£6,240 to £50,270 in 2026/27): 8% total, of which at least 3% from you, the employee usually paying 4% plus 1% tax relief.
- Pay contributions in by the 22nd of the following month (19th by cheque), complete the declaration of compliance with The Pensions Regulator, and re-enrol every 3 years. Keep records for 6 years.

## Employment Rights Act 2025: the timetable as published

The government's timeline (updated 25/09/2026) says all future dates may change.

| Date | Change that matters to a small employer |
|---|---|
| December 2025 | Royal Assent |
| 06/04/2026 | Day-one Paternity Leave and Unpaid Parental Leave; SSP from day one with no Lower Earnings Limit; stronger whistleblowing protection on sexual harassment |
| 07/04/2026 | Fair Work Agency set up (enforces pay and holiday rights) |
| 01/10/2026 | Employment tribunal claim time limit rises from 3 to 6 months |
| 30/10/2026 | Duty to take "all reasonable steps" to prevent sexual harassment, including by third parties such as customers |
| By end of 2026 | Tipping law strengthened |
| January 2027 | Unfair dismissal qualifying period cut to 6 months for dismissals from 01/01/2027, compensation cap removed; fire and rehire protections; duty to tell workers of their right to join a union |
| 2027 (dates to follow) | Guaranteed hours and shift notice rights, flexible working changes, bereavement leave including pregnancy loss, stronger protection for pregnant women and new mothers, NDA restrictions, umbrella company regulation |

Practical upshot: review probation before 6 months, and have an anti-harassment policy by 30/10/2026. Track changes in [changes ledger](changes-ledger.md).
