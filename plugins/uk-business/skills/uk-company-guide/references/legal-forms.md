# UK business legal forms

> Reference, not legal, tax or accounting advice. Figures researched against the official sources listed at the end on 06/10/2026; key figures re-checked on gov.uk on 16/10/2026. Confidence: high. Re-check by 06/04/2027, or sooner after a Budget or Spring Statement.

For first-time and side-hustle founders in the UK deciding how to set up: sole trader, ordinary partnership, limited liability partnership (LLP) or private limited company. This is reference material, not legal or tax advice. Check your own situation with an accountant before committing.

## The four common forms at a glance

| | Sole trader | Ordinary partnership | LLP | Private limited company (by shares) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Liability | Unlimited. You are personally responsible for all business debts | Unlimited. Partners personally share losses and bills | Members are not personally liable for debts the LLP cannot pay | Limited. Owners are liable only up to the value of their investment (unpaid share value) |
| Tax in one line | Income Tax and National Insurance on profits via Self Assessment | Each partner pays tax on their share of profits; nominated partner files a partnership return | Taxed like a partnership: each member pays tax on their share | Company pays Corporation Tax on profits; you pay personal tax on salary or dividends you take out |
| Who you register with | HMRC only (Self Assessment) | HMRC only (partnership and each partner) | Companies House, then HMRC | Companies House, then HMRC |
| Set-up cost (government fee) | £0 | £0 | £100 by software, £124 on paper | £100 online or by software, £124 on paper |
| Admin load | Lowest. Keep records, file a Self Assessment return | Low to medium. Partnership return plus a personal return for every partner | High. Annual accounts and confirmation statement at Companies House, plus returns | High. Annual accounts, confirmation statement (£50 online), Company Tax Return, payroll if you pay a salary |
| What is public | No public register of sole traders | No public register. Your names must appear on invoices and letters | Name, registered address, members, PSCs and filed documents on the Companies House register | Name, registered office, directors (name and service address), PSCs, shareholders and filed documents including accounts |
| Minimum people | 1 | 2 | 2 designated members | 1 (one person can be sole shareholder and director) |

Fees are Companies House fees from 01/02/2026. See [registration and identifiers](registration-and-identifiers.md) for how to register each one.

## Sole trader

The simplest structure. You are self-employed, make all decisions and keep all profits after tax. You can be employed and self-employed at the same time, which is why most side hustles start here. You can start trading straight away but must register for Self Assessment once you earn more than £1,000 in a tax year (the trading allowance). A trading name must not include "Limited", "Ltd", "LLP" or "plc", and your own name must appear on invoices and letters.

Typically chosen by: solo founders testing an idea, freelancers, side hustles, and anyone whose main risk can be covered by insurance.

## Ordinary partnership

Two or more people (a limited company can also be a partner) run a business together and personally share responsibility for losses and bills. You choose a "nominated partner" who files the partnership tax return and keeps records; every partner also files their own Self Assessment return. There is no registration at Companies House. A written partnership agreement is not a GOV.UK requirement, but without one you have no agreed rules for profit split or exit.

Typically chosen by: two friends or family members trading together cheaply, where the risk of debts is low.

## Limited liability partnership (LLP)

A partnership with limited liability, registered at Companies House. It needs at least 2 "designated members" at all times, who keep accounting records, file annual accounts and report changes. Each member pays tax on their share of profits, as in an ordinary partnership. GOV.UK says you should make an LLP agreement covering profit share, decisions and how members join or leave. The name must end in "Limited Liability Partnership" or "LLP".

Typically chosen by: professional firms and multi-founder service businesses that want limited liability but partnership-style taxation. Rarely the right fit for a solo founder or a startup seeking equity investment.

## Private limited company (limited by shares)

A company is legally separate from the people who own it. It needs at least one director (aged 16 or over) and at least one shareholder, who can be the same person. Shareholders' liability is limited to what they invested, which is why many companies issue shares at a low value such as £1. The company pays Corporation Tax on profits, and directors must follow rules when taking money out (salary, dividends or a director's loan). Company money must be kept separate from personal money.

Typically chosen by: founders who want limited liability, plan to raise investment by selling shares, are making steady profits, or work with clients who prefer to contract with a company. See [corporation tax](corporation-tax.md), [company annual compliance](company-annual-compliance.md) and [paying yourself as a director](paying-yourself-as-a-director.md).

## Choosing

### When staying a sole trader makes sense

- You are testing an idea or running a side hustle with modest income.
- Your business carries little risk of large debts or claims, or insurance covers it.
- You want the least admin and cost, and no public register entry.
- You may stay below the £1,000 trading allowance at first, in which case you may not need to register at all.

### When incorporating is worth considering

The usual drivers, none of which is a legal threshold:

- **Liability**: you are taking on leases, stock, staff, large contracts or anything where a claim could exceed what insurance covers. Note that banks and landlords often ask directors for personal guarantees, which bypass limited liability.
- **Profit level**: a common rule of thumb among accountants is that a company can become more tax efficient than sole trading once profits are steady and comfortably above what you need to live on, because you can choose when to draw money. This is a rule of thumb, not law, and the break-even point moves every time tax rates change. Get it calculated for your figures. See [personal tax and national insurance](personal-tax-and-national-insurance.md).
- **Investment**: equity investors buy shares, which needs a company. Sole traders and partnerships cannot issue shares.
- **Credibility**: some larger clients and agencies prefer or require suppliers to be limited companies.

### It is reversible, but not free

GOV.UK notes you can move from one structure to another, and that moving from sole trader to limited company is usually easier than the reverse. Costs to expect:

- Incorporating: £100 Companies House fee, a new bank account, new contracts and possibly new VAT and payroll registrations.
- Running a company: accountant fees are usually higher, plus a £50 annual confirmation statement fee online.
- Closing a company: a voluntary strike off costs £13 online (£18 on paper), but you must first deal with the company's assets, tax and debts. Getting money back out of a company can trigger tax, so take advice before closing.

### What to do

1. If you are just starting and the risk is low, register as a sole trader once you pass £1,000 of trading income.
2. Review the structure with an accountant when profits become steady, when you take on real liability, or before you raise money.
3. If you have a co-founder, write down who owns what and how either of you can leave, whatever structure you pick.
