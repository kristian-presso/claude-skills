# VAT schemes, Making Tax Digital and returns

> Reference, not legal, tax or accounting advice. Figures researched against the official sources listed at the end on 06/10/2026; key figures re-checked on gov.uk on 16/10/2026. Confidence: high. Re-check by 06/01/2027, or sooner after a Budget or Spring Statement.

This page is for UK founders who are already VAT registered, or about to be. It covers the three small-business VAT schemes, Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT, return and payment deadlines, penalties and interest, record keeping and what a VAT invoice must show. It is reference, not advice. For whether to register at all, see [vat registration and thresholds](vat-registration-and-thresholds.md).

## The three small-business schemes at a glance

| Scheme | Join if taxable turnover is | Must leave if | Main effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat Rate Scheme | £150,000 or less a year (excluding VAT) expected in the next 12 months | Turnover over £230,000 (including VAT) in the last 12 months at your joining anniversary, or expected in the next 12 months or the next 30 days alone | Pay a fixed % of your VAT-inclusive turnover. No reclaim on purchases except some capital assets over £2,000 |
| Cash Accounting Scheme | £1.35 million or less expected in the next 12 months | Turnover over £1.6 million | Pay VAT when customers pay you. Reclaim VAT when you pay suppliers |
| Annual Accounting Scheme | £1.35 million or less expected in the next 12 months | Turnover over £1.6 million (or likely to be) at the end of your accounting year | One return a year, with advance payments during the year |

You cannot use the Flat Rate Scheme and the Cash Accounting Scheme together (the Flat Rate Scheme has its own cash-based turnover option). You can combine the Flat Rate and Annual Accounting schemes, but you must apply for each separately. After leaving the Flat Rate or Annual Accounting scheme, you must wait 12 months before rejoining.

### Flat Rate Scheme (FRS)

- Your rate depends on your business sector. Examples: management consultancy 14%, computer and IT consultancy 14.5%, advertising 11%, photography 11%, "business services not listed elsewhere" 12%.
- **First-year discount:** 1% off your flat rate in your first year as a VAT-registered business.
- **Limited cost trader rule:** if your spending on goods is less than 2% of turnover, or more than 2% but under £1,000 a year, you must use 16.5%. Only some purchases count as "goods" for this test, so use HMRC's calculator and its list of what counts before applying.
- **How the maths works:** you charge customers 20% as normal, then pay HMRC your flat % of the gross figure. GOV.UK example: a photographer bills £1,000 + £200 VAT = £1,200 and pays 11% of £1,200, which is £132.
- **Who it suits:** low-cost businesses not caught by the 16.5% rate. A pure-services founder on 16.5% often gains little or nothing.

### Cash Accounting Scheme

You do not need to tell HMRC. Just start at the beginning of a VAT period. It helps cash flow when customers pay late, because you do not pay VAT on unpaid invoices. It cannot be used for invoices with payment terms of 6 months or more, invoices raised in advance, or hire purchase deals. When you leave, you must account for all outstanding VAT, which you can usually spread over 6 months.

### Annual Accounting Scheme

You pay in advance (9 monthly payments of 10% or 3 quarterly payments of 25% of your estimated bill), then file one return and pay the balance within 2 months of your year end. It does not suit businesses that usually get refunds, as you get only one a year.

## Making Tax Digital for VAT

- **Who:** all VAT-registered businesses, including voluntary registrations. HMRC signs new registrations up automatically. Exemption is only for those who cannot reasonably go digital (for example because of age, disability, location, religious objection or no internet access). Apply by phone or letter.
- **Software:** keep records and file returns from MTD-compatible software, or from a spreadsheet plus "bridging" software. Use HMRC's software finder. The software's authority to talk to HMRC lasts 18 months.
- **Digital links:** data moving between products must move digitally (formulas, CSV or XML import and export, file upload). Retyping or copy-and-paste is not allowed.
- Sending a paper return when you are not exempt can bring a penalty of up to £400.

## Return periods and deadlines

- Returns are usually quarterly. HMRC also recognises monthly periods and annual periods (through the Annual Accounting Scheme). You can see and change your return dates in your VAT online account.
- **Deadline: 1 calendar month and 7 days after the end of the period, for both filing and payment.** Example: for the quarter ending 31/03, file and pay by 07/05. The money must reach HMRC by then, even if that day falls on a weekend or bank holiday.
- You must file even if you have nothing to pay (a nil return).

## Paying

- **Direct Debit** (set up in your VAT online account): set it up at least 3 working days before you submit the return. HMRC then collects 3 working days after the deadline, or 3 days after you file if you file late.
- **Bank transfer:** Faster Payments usually arrive the same or next day, CHAPS the same working day, Bacs in 3 working days.
- **Card:** treated as paid on the day. Personal credit cards are not accepted, and corporate cards carry a fee.

Use your 9-digit VAT number, with no spaces, as the payment reference.

## Penalties and interest

These apply to VAT periods starting on or after 01/01/2023.

**Late submission: points system.** Each late return earns 1 point. When you reach the threshold for your filing frequency, you get a £200 penalty, plus another £200 for every further late return while you stay at the threshold. Late nil returns count. Your first return after registering does not.

| Filing frequency | Points threshold | To reset points: file on time for |
|---|---|---|
| Annual | 2 | 24 months (2 returns) |
| Quarterly | 4 | 12 months (4 returns) |
| Monthly | 5 | 6 months (6 returns) |

To reset, you must also have submitted every outstanding return from the previous 24 months. If you are below the threshold, each point expires automatically after about 24 months.

**Late payment penalties** (rates in force since 01/04/2025):

| Days overdue | Penalty |
|---|---|
| 1 to 15 | None |
| 16 to 30 | 3% of what was unpaid at day 15 |
| 31 or more | 3% of what was unpaid at day 15 plus 3% of what was unpaid at day 30, plus a second penalty charged daily at 10% a year until you pay |

Agreeing a Time to Pay arrangement early can reduce or stop these penalties.

**Late payment interest:** charged from the first day overdue at Bank of England base rate plus 4%. That is **7.75% from 09/01/2026**. HMRC repayment interest is base rate minus 1% (2.75%). These rates move whenever the base rate changes, so check the live table before quoting.

## Record keeping

Keep VAT records for at least **6 years**, or 10 years if you use the VAT One Stop Shop. That includes all sales invoices (even cancelled ones), purchase invoices, credit and debit notes, and a VAT account summarising the VAT you owe and can reclaim. Under MTD, the VAT on sales and purchases, the time and value of each supply, adjustments and reverse-charge entries must be held digitally.

## What must be on a VAT invoice

You must give a VAT invoice to VAT-registered customers for standard-rated and reduced-rated supplies, normally within 30 days of the tax point. A full VAT invoice shows:

1. A unique sequential invoice number
2. The time of supply (tax point), and the issue date if different
3. Your name, address and VAT registration number
4. The customer's name and address
5. A description of the goods or services
6. For each line: quantity or extent, VAT rate and amount excluding VAT
7. Total amount excluding VAT
8. Rate of any cash discount offered
9. Total VAT, in sterling
10. Unit price (for example an hourly rate) where relevant

Show any zero-rated or exempt items clearly as carrying no VAT, with a separate total. **Simplified invoice** (£250 or less, if the customer agrees): your name, address and VAT number, the tax point, a description, and for each VAT rate the VAT-inclusive total and the rate.

## What to do

1. Pick schemes at registration: test the Flat Rate Scheme against the 16.5% rule, and consider Cash Accounting if customers pay slowly.
2. Choose MTD software before your first return is due.
3. Set up a Direct Debit at least 3 working days before your first filing.
4. Put every "1 month and 7 days" deadline in [key deadlines calendar](key-deadlines-calendar.md).
5. If you cannot pay, contact HMRC before day 15.
