# Data protection and marketing rules in the UK

> Reference, not legal, tax or accounting advice. Figures researched against the official sources listed at the end on 06/10/2026; key figures re-checked on gov.uk on 16/10/2026. Confidence: medium. Re-check by 06/01/2027, or sooner after a Budget or Spring Statement.

For UK founders who collect customer, subscriber or staff details and want to market by email, text or phone. Two sets of rules apply together: UK GDPR with the Data Protection Act 2018 (how you handle personal data), and the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations, PECR (marketing messages, calls and cookies). The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA) amended all three. This is reference material, not legal advice.

## What changed in 2025 and 2026

The DUAA received Royal Assent on 19/06/2025. Most data protection and PECR changes came into force on 05/02/2026 (including Schedule 13 on PECR fines, commenced by S.I. 2026/82, per legislation.gov.uk, checked 16/10/2026), and the ICO confirmed on 19/06/2026 that all data protection provisions are now in force, including the new complaints duty. In short:

- A new lawful basis, "recognised legitimate interests", for a fixed list of purposes.
- Some cookies no longer need consent (see below).
- Charities get their own email soft opt-in.
- You must run a data protection complaints process.
- PECR fines are aligned with UK GDPR fines: up to £17.5 million or 4% of worldwide turnover (in force since 05/02/2026).
- Subject access searches need only be "reasonable and proportionate".

## UK GDPR basics for a small business

**1. Pick a lawful basis before you use the data, and write it down.** There are now seven:

| Basis | Typical small-business use |
|---|---|
| Consent | Newsletter sign-up, non-essential cookies |
| Contract | Processing an order, delivering a service |
| Legal obligation | Payroll records, HMRC, right to work copies |
| Vital interests | Protecting someone's life (rare) |
| Public task | Public bodies (rare for you) |
| Recognised legitimate interest | Only for listed purposes: safeguarding vulnerable people, emergencies, crime, national or public security, and requests from bodies that need data for a public task |
| Legitimate interests | Fraud prevention, B2B prospecting, marketing to existing customers. You must balance your interest against the person's |

Do not swap bases later without good reason, especially away from consent.

**2. Publish a privacy notice** saying who you are, what you collect, why, your lawful basis, who you share with, how long you keep it, any transfers abroad, people's rights and how to complain to you and to the ICO.

**3. Respect people's rights:** access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability, objection (absolute for direct marketing) and rights around automated decisions. Answer a subject access request without undue delay and within one month. You can extend by up to 2 more months for complex requests if you tell the person within the first month. Searches must be reasonable and proportionate.

**4. Handle complaints.** Since the DUAA you must help people complain to you (for example an online form), acknowledge within 30 days and respond without undue delay.

**5. Report serious breaches.** If a breach is likely to risk people's rights, tell the ICO as soon as possible and where feasible within 72 hours of becoming aware. If the risk is high, tell the affected people without undue delay too. Log every breach, reported or not.

**6. Pay the ICO data protection fee** unless exempt. Tiers, exemptions and penalties are in [registration and identifiers](registration-and-identifiers.md).

## PECR: marketing by email and text

| Recipient | Unsolicited email or SMS marketing allowed? |
|---|---|
| Individual subscriber (people, sole traders, ordinary partnerships) | Only with consent, or if you meet a soft opt-in |
| Corporate subscriber (limited companies, LLPs, Scottish partnerships) | Yes, without consent, but you must identify yourself and give a working unsubscribe |

Consent must be freely given, specific, informed and an active opt-in. No pre-ticked boxes and no bundling into your terms.

**Products and services soft opt-in.** You can email or text without consent only if all of these are true:

- you collected the details yourself
- you did so while selling or negotiating to sell
- you market only your own similar products or services
- you offered an opt-out when you collected the details
- every message includes an opt-out

Bought-in lists can never rely on the soft opt-in.

**Charity soft opt-in (new under the DUAA).** Charities can email people who supported or expressed an interest in their work, with an opt-out at collection and in every message.

Every marketing message must say who it is from and give a valid unsubscribe address, whether solicited or not. Personal data in B2B lists is still covered by UK GDPR.

## Cold calling

- Screen live marketing calls against the Telephone Preference Service (TPS) and Corporate TPS (CTPS). Do not call registered numbers unless that person specifically consented to your calls, even existing customers.
- Keep your own do-not-call list and honour objections.
- Always say who is calling and display your number.
- Automated (recorded message) marketing calls need specific consent for that type of call.
- Claims management calls need consent. Pension scheme cold calls are banned except under strict conditions.

## Cookies and similar technologies

You still need consent before setting non-essential cookies, pixels or similar trackers. Since the DUAA there are five exceptions where consent is not needed:

1. **Communication:** only to transmit a communication.
2. **Strictly necessary:** for the service the user asked for, for example a shopping basket, login session or security. No advertising purpose qualifies.
3. **Statistical purposes:** analytics used only to measure and improve your own service.
4. **Appearance:** remembering how the user wants your site to look or work.
5. **Emergency assistance:** location to provide emergency help.

For the statistical and appearance exceptions you must tell people and give a simple, free way to object. Advertising and cross-site tracking still need opt-in consent, with no pre-enabled boxes. If you use a third-party analytics provider under the statistical exception, it may only help you improve your own service, the output must be aggregate data that cannot identify people, and you must tell users who the provider is and what it does. Check this before you drop analytics from your banner.

## Fines

| Regime | Maximum |
|---|---|
| UK GDPR and DPA 2018, higher tier (principles, rights, transfers) | £17.5 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher |
| UK GDPR and DPA 2018, standard tier (other obligations) | £8.7 million or 2% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher |
| PECR (marketing, cookies) | Since 05/02/2026, the higher maximum above (£17.5 million or 4%). DUAA Schedule 13 applies it to breaches of the main PECR rules (regulations 5 to 8, 14, 19 to 24), including cookies, calls and email marketing |

Before the DUAA the PECR cap was £500,000. As of 16/10/2026 the ICO's "What are PECR?" page still quotes that figure and says it is under review; the Act, not that page, sets the current maximum. The ICO also has new powers to compel interviews and require you to pay for technical reports.

## International transfers in two lines

Sending personal data to a separate organisation outside the UK (including many SaaS tools) is a "restricted transfer". It needs UK adequacy regulations for that country, or a safeguard such as the ICO's IDTA or the UK Addendum with a transfer risk assessment, or a narrow exception.

## Quick checklist

- Map what data you hold, its lawful basis and how long you keep it.
- Publish a privacy notice and a complaints route.
- Check your sign-up forms: unticked opt-in boxes, or soft opt-in wording with an opt-out.
- Configure your cookie banner against the five exceptions.
- Write a breach plan with the 72-hour clock.
- Pay or check exemption from the ICO fee.
