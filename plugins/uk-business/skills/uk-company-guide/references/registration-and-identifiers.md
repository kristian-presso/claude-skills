## Registering as self-employed (sole trader)

You register as a sole trader by registering for Self Assessment with HMRC. You must if you earn more than £1,000 in a tax year (6 April to 5 April); you can register earlier. You need a National Insurance number and HMRC online sign-in details (Government Gateway user ID or GOV.UK One Login; new users choose "Create new sign in details").

**Deadline**: by 5 October after the end of the tax year in which you need to file. GOV.UK states 05/10/2026 for the 2025-26 tax year (06/04/2025 to 05/04/2026), so that date has just passed; the same rule points to 05/10/2027 for 2026-27. Late registration can mean a penalty.

**What you get**: a UTR for your returns. If you are already in Self Assessment for another reason, you still register again as a sole trader so you are set up for Class 2 National Insurance. See [self assessment](self-assessment.md) and [making tax digital income tax](making-tax-digital-income-tax.md).

## Registering a company at Companies House

**Fee from 01/02/2026**: £100 online or by software (usually registered within 24 hours), £156 same day by software, £124 by post on form IN01 (8 to 10 days). Online, you create a separate Government Gateway ID for the company and are usually set up for Corporation Tax at the same time.

### What you need

- **Name**: usually ending "Limited" or "Ltd"; not the "same as" an existing name; no offensive or unapproved sensitive words. Check the register first. A "too like" name may have to change after a complaint.
- **Registered office**: a physical UK address in the country of registration (England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland), where post reaches someone and delivery can be acknowledged. No PO Boxes. It is public, so use an accountant's or agent's address (with permission) to keep your home off the register.
- **Registered email address**: required, not published.
- **SIC code**: from the Companies House condensed list only, or the filing may be rejected.
- **Directors**: at least one, aged 16 or over. Names and service addresses are public. A company secretary is optional.
- **Shareholders and share capital**: at least one shareholder (can be the director), set out in a statement of capital with share numbers, values and rights. Example: 100 shares at £1 is £100 share capital.
- **PSCs**: usually anyone with more than 25% of shares or votes, power to appoint or remove most directors, or significant influence or control. Home addresses are not disclosed.
- **Memorandum and articles**: online, the memorandum is created for you; use the "model articles" or upload your own.

**Company Registration Number**: shown on the certificate of incorporation. Your registered number, registered office, place of registration and limited status must appear on business letters, order forms and your website.

## Companies House identity verification

Introduced under the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act. Voluntary from 08/04/2025, a legal requirement since 18/11/2025.

**Who must verify now**: directors (and equivalents such as LLP members), PSCs, and authorised agents (ACSPs). Verify once, free, through GOV.UK One Login (app, online questions or Post Office), or pay an ACSP such as an accountant. You receive a personal code.

### When

- New company: every director's code at registration.
- Existing directors: with the company's next confirmation statement.
- PSC who is also a director: within 14 days starting the day after the confirmation statement date.
- PSC registered before 18/11/2025 who is not a director: first 14 days of their birth month.
- PSC added after 18/11/2025: on being added or within 14 days.

**Not yet required**: people who only file, limited partnerships, corporate directors, corporate LLP members and officers of corporate PSCs ("at a later date"). Not verifying can lead to prosecution, fines and a public note on the register.

## Corporation Tax registration

A company must notify HMRC no later than 3 months after its first accounting period begins (Finance Act 2004, section 55). In practice that starts when it begins to do business: buying, selling, advertising, renting property or employing someone. If not set up during online incorporation, add Corporation Tax to your business tax account using the company number, start date, accounts date and the 10-digit UTR. Request the UTR online if it has not arrived 15 working days after registering. See [corporation tax](corporation-tax.md).

## ICO data protection fee

Organisations and sole traders using personal information must pay unless exempt. Fees since 17/02/2025:

| Tier | Who | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Micro | Turnover up to £632,000 or no more than 10 staff | £52 |
| 2 Small and medium | Turnover up to £36 million or no more than 250 staff | £78 |
| 3 Large | Everyone else | £3,763 |

Direct debit gives £5 off. **Exempt** if you process personal data only for staff administration; advertising, marketing and PR; accounts and records; not-for-profit purposes; personal or household affairs; a public register; judicial functions; or without an automated system. Confirm with the ICO self-assessment (about 10 minutes). Maximum penalty for not paying: £4,350. See [data protection and marketing](data-protection-and-marketing.md).

## Business bank account

- **Limited company**: company and personal money must be kept separate because the company is a separate legal entity. GOV.UK says a business bank account is the simplest way.
- **Sole trader**: we found no GOV.UK rule requiring a separate account. One still makes records and returns easier; check your bank's terms on business use of personal accounts.

## Trade marks (UK IPO)

Fees from 01/04/2026 (up from £170 online):

- Online: £205 for one class, plus £60 per extra class.
- Right Start: £125 plus £30 per extra class for a pre-check, then a further £125 (plus £30 per class) to continue.
- Paper: £250 for one class, plus £60 per extra class.

A class is a broad category (class 25 is clothing). Only pick classes and terms you plan to use within 5 years. Unopposed registration usually takes 3 to 4 months, lasts 10 years and covers the UK and Isle of Man only.

## Domain and brand basics

Check the Companies House register, the trade mark database and domain availability together. Applications are published on GOV.UK as soon as you apply, so secure the domain first. A company or trading name gives no trade mark rights.
