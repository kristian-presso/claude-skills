# Self Assessment for sole traders and directors

> Reference, not legal, tax or accounting advice. Figures researched against the official sources listed at the end on 06/10/2026; key figures re-checked on gov.uk on 16/10/2026. Confidence: high. Re-check by 06/01/2027, or sooner after a Budget or Spring Statement.

This page is for UK sole traders, company directors and people with side-hustle income on top of a job. It covers who must file a Self Assessment return, the key dates, payments on account, penalties and the main rules for working out profit. It is reference, not advice; check unusual situations with HMRC or an accountant.

The return currently being prepared is for the 2025-26 tax year (06/04/2025 to 05/04/2026). It is due online by 31/01/2027.

## Who must register and file

GOV.UK says you must send a return if, in the tax year:

- you were self-employed as a sole trader and earned more than £1,000 (the trading allowance, see below)
- you were a partner in a business partnership
- you had Capital Gains Tax to pay
- you had to pay the High Income Child Benefit Charge and do not pay it through PAYE
- HMRC wrote to you asking for a return

You may also need to file for other untaxed income such as rent, tips, dividends, savings interest or foreign income.

**Company directors.** Being a director does not automatically mean you must file. HMRC's director guidance says you need a return if you receive dividends or have other untaxed income on top of your director's salary. If you are a director who does file, from 2025-26 you must generally report each directorship on an SA102 Employment page, even where you had no pay or shareholding that year. If unsure, use HMRC's "check if you need to send a tax return" tool.

## Registering

- Register by **5 October** after the end of the tax year you need to report. For 2025-26 that was 05/10/2026.
- This applies if you have never filed before, or you filed before but did not need to for 2024-25.
- Registering late can bring a penalty. If you register after 05/10/2026, you get 3 months from the date of HMRC's letter or email to file.
- Once registered you get a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR), which you need for every return.

## Payments on account

Payments on account are advance payments towards next year's bill.

- **When they apply:** you pay them unless last year's Self Assessment bill was under £1,000, or more than 80% of last year's tax was already paid at source (for example through your tax code).
- **How much:** each payment is usually half of last year's bill.
- **When:** 31 January and 31 July.
- **Balancing payment:** if the final bill is higher than the two payments, you pay the rest by 31 January the following year. GOV.UK's example: a £3,000 bill with two payments of £900 leaves a £1,200 balancing payment.
- **Reducing them:** if you expect lower profits, use "Reduce payments on account" in your HMRC online account, or send form SA303. If you reduce them too far, HMRC charges interest on the shortfall.

First-year shock: on one 31 January you may pay a whole year's tax plus half of it again as a payment on account.

## Penalties (if you are not yet in Making Tax Digital)

### Late filing

| How late | Penalty |
|---|---|
| 1 day | £100, even if no tax is due |
| 3 months | £10 a day, up to £900 |
| 6 months | further 5% of tax due or £300, whichever is greater |
| 12 months | another 5% or £300, whichever is greater |

**Late payment:** 5% of the unpaid tax at 30 days, at 6 months and at 12 months, plus interest. HMRC's late payment interest rate is the Bank of England base rate plus 4%; it was 7.75% from 09/01/2026. Check the HMRC interest page for the current figure.

If you are in Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, a different points-based regime applies from 2026-27. See [making tax digital income tax](making-tax-digital-income-tax.md).

## The £1,000 trading allowance

- If your gross trading income (before expenses) is £1,000 or less in a tax year, you do not need to tell HMRC or pay tax on it ("full relief").
- If it is over £1,000, you can deduct £1,000 instead of your actual expenses ("partial relief"). This helps only if your real expenses are under £1,000.
- You cannot use it for income from a company you control, a partnership you are in, or your employer.

## Working out profit

**Cash basis is now the standard.** From 06/04/2024, cash basis is the default for sole traders and partnerships without corporate partners. You record income when you receive it and expenses when you pay them. There is no turnover limit. If you want traditional (accruals) accounting instead, you say so on your return. Limited companies, LLPs and some others cannot use cash basis.

**Allowable expenses** reduce your taxable profit. GOV.UK lists office costs, travel, uniforms, staff and subcontractors, stock and raw materials, insurance and bank charges, business premises costs, advertising and marketing, professional subscriptions and business training. Money taken out for personal use is not an expense. For mixed-use items claim only the business share: GOV.UK's example is £200 of phone bills of which £70 is business, so you claim £70. You cannot claim expenses if you use the £1,000 trading allowance instead.

**Simplified expenses** (sole traders and partnerships without company partners) let you use flat rates instead of working out actual costs:

| Item | Flat rate |
|---|---|
| Car or goods vehicle, first 10,000 business miles (2026-27) | 55p a mile |
| Car or goods vehicle, after 10,000 miles (2026-27) | 25p a mile |
| Car or goods vehicle, first 10,000 miles (before 06/04/2026) | 45p a mile |
| Motorcycle | 24p a mile |
| Working from home 25 to 50 hours a month | £10 a month |
| Working from home 51 to 100 hours a month | £18 a month |
| Working from home 101 hours or more a month | £26 a month |

Once you use flat rates for a vehicle you must keep using them for that vehicle. Phone and internet are claimed at actual business cost.

## Basis period reform: tax-year basis

From 2024-25, sole traders and partners are taxed on the profit arising in the tax year itself (06/04 to 05/04), whatever their accounting date. 2023-24 was the transition year. Any extra "transition profit" from that year is spread over five years by default unless you elect to speed it up. With any other accounting date you apportion profits from two sets of accounts into the tax year, so a 31 March or 5 April year end is simplest.

## Record keeping

- Keep records of all sales and income, business expenses, VAT and PAYE records if relevant, and personal income.
- Keep proof: receipts, bank statements, sales invoices, till rolls.
- Keep them for **at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline** of the tax year. Example: a 2025-26 return due 31/01/2027 means keeping records until at least 31/01/2032.
- If you are in Making Tax Digital, records must be digital. See [making tax digital income tax](making-tax-digital-income-tax.md).

## What to do now

1. Check whether you need to file using HMRC's tool, and register by 5 October if you do.
2. Set aside tax and National Insurance from every payment you receive. Rates are in [personal tax and national insurance](personal-tax-and-national-insurance.md).
3. Put the dates above in your calendar. See [key deadlines calendar](key-deadlines-calendar.md).
4. If you are a director, see [paying yourself as a director](paying-yourself-as-a-director.md).
