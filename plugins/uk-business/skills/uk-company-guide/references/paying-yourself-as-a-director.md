# Paying yourself as a company director

> Reference, not legal, tax or accounting advice. Figures researched against the official sources listed at the end on 06/10/2026; key figures re-checked on gov.uk on 16/10/2026. Confidence: high. Re-check by 06/01/2027, or sooner after a Budget or Spring Statement.

For founders who own and run their own UK limited company and want to understand the main ways of taking money out: salary, dividends, pension contributions, director's loans and expenses. Figures are for the 2026-27 tax year (06/04/2026 to 05/04/2027) and use England and Northern Ireland income tax bands; Scotland and Wales differ. This is reference material, not advice: the right mix depends on your other income, your company's profit and your plans, so check with an accountant. Company money is not your money until it is paid to you in one of these ways.

## The options at a glance

| Route | Company Corporation Tax | Your tax | Paperwork |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salary | Deductible (salary and employer NI) | Income tax and employee NI through PAYE | Payroll and reporting to HMRC |
| Dividends | Not deductible: paid from profit after tax | Dividend tax above the £500 allowance | Board minutes, dividend vouchers |
| Employer pension contribution | Usually deductible if part of a remuneration package paid wholly for the business | None when paid in, within allowances | Pension scheme |
| Director's loan | Not a cost; possible section 455 charge | Possible benefit in kind | Loan account records |
| Expenses and benefits | Business costs deductible | Benefits may be taxable | Receipts, P11D where relevant |

## Salary and PAYE

You must register the company as an employer with HMRC **before the first payday**, even if you are only paying yourself as the only director. You cannot register more than 2 months before you start paying people. After registering, enrol for PAYE Online and run payroll each time you pay a salary.

### 2026-27 rates and thresholds

| Item | Annual figure |
|---|---|
| Personal Allowance | £12,570 |
| Basic rate income tax 20% | Next £37,700 (to £50,270) |
| Higher rate 40% | £50,271 to £125,140 |
| Additional rate 45% | Above £125,140 |
| Lower earnings limit (LEL) | £6,708 |
| Secondary threshold (employer NI starts) | £5,000 |
| Primary threshold (employee NI starts) | £12,570 |
| Upper earnings limit | £50,270 |
| Employee Class 1 NI (category A) | 8% between £12,570 and £50,270, 2% above |
| Employer Class 1 NI | **15%** above £5,000 |
| Class 1A NI on benefits | 15% |

Directors have an **annual earnings period** for NI, so NI is worked out on total pay for the year rather than each month. Many directors use HMRC's "alternative arrangements", paying NI monthly as normal and adjusting at the year end. HMRC booklet CA44 explains both methods.

### Employment Allowance

Eligible employers can reduce their employer NI bill by up to **£10,500** in 2026-27. Since April 2025 there is no £100,000 cap on previous-year NI. **A company with one director cannot claim if that director is the only employee liable for employer NI.** So a one-person company usually gets no Employment Allowance; having another employee who is liable for employer NI may change that. See [employing people](employing-people.md).

## Common salary approaches (with trade-offs)

These are approaches founders and accountants commonly discuss, not recommendations. All assume no other employment income.

| Approach | Annual salary | Employer NI (no Employment Allowance) | Employee NI and tax | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Secondary threshold | £5,000 | £0 | £0 | Below the £6,708 LEL, so it does not count towards a State Pension qualifying year |
| Lower earnings limit | £6,708 | £256.20 | £0 | Earns a qualifying year with small employer NI; uses less of your Personal Allowance |
| Personal Allowance / primary threshold | £12,570 | £1,135.50 | £0 | Uses the full Personal Allowance and gets bigger Corporation Tax relief, but costs employer NI unless Employment Allowance applies |

Salary and employer NI reduce company profit, saving Corporation Tax at 19% to 26.5% (see [corporation tax](corporation-tax.md)). Whether the extra employer NI is worth it depends on your profit level and whether you qualify for Employment Allowance. Salary also counts as earnings for the limit on tax relief for your own pension contributions; dividends do not.

## Dividends

### Rules

- A company must not pay out more in dividends than its **available (distributable) profits** from current and previous financial years. Check the latest management accounts before declaring.
- Dividends are not a business cost for Corporation Tax.
- You must usually pay dividends to all shareholders (of the relevant share class).

### Paperwork

1. Hold a directors' meeting to **declare** the dividend and keep minutes, even if you are the only director.
2. Write a **dividend voucher** for each payment showing the date, company name, shareholder names and amount.
3. Give a copy to each recipient and keep a copy with the company records.

**Dividend tax, 2026-27** (06/04/2026 to 05/04/2027):

| Band | Rate on dividends above the £500 allowance |
|---|---|
| Basic rate | 10.75% |
| Higher rate | 35.75% |
| Additional rate | 39.35% |

Dividends are added on top of your other income to decide the band. Dividends inside your unused Personal Allowance are tax free. Over the allowances you must tell HMRC; with more than £10,000 of dividends you must file Self Assessment (register by 05/10 after the tax year). See [self assessment](self-assessment.md) and [personal tax and national insurance](personal-tax-and-national-insurance.md).

## Pension contributions

Employer contributions from the company to a registered pension scheme are usually an allowable expense for Corporation Tax, as long as the overall pay package is reasonable for the work done and there is no non-trade purpose. Total contributions are subject to your **£60,000 annual allowance** (it can be lower for some people). Tax relief on your own personal contributions is limited to 100% of your earnings, so a low salary limits the relief on contributions you make personally.

## Director's loans

Taking money that is not salary, dividends or a repaid expense creates a director's loan. If it is still outstanding 9 months after the company's year end, the company pays a temporary section 455 charge of **35.75%** on loans made in 2026-27 (refundable after repayment). Loans over £10,000 are a taxable benefit. Full rules are in [company annual compliance](company-annual-compliance.md).

## Expenses and benefits

- The company can reimburse genuine business expenses with receipts.
- Anything you get personal use from is a benefit: report it, and the company pays 15% Class 1A NI on it.
- **Trivial benefits** of £50 or less each are tax free, but directors of close companies are capped at **£300** a tax year.
- Mileage for business journeys in your own car can be paid without reporting to HMRC up to the approved rates: for 2026-27, **55p a mile** for the first 10,000 business miles and 25p after that for tax (55p for all miles for NI).

## What to do

1. Register for PAYE before the first salary payment and pick payroll software.
2. Choose a salary level with your accountant, knowing the trade-offs above.
3. Check distributable profits before every dividend, then minute it and issue vouchers.
4. Keep the director's loan account at or near zero at the year end.
5. Diarise Self Assessment and payroll dates in [key deadlines calendar](key-deadlines-calendar.md).
