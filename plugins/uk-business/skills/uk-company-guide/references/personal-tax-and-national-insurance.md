# Personal tax and National Insurance 2026-27

> Reference, not legal, tax or accounting advice. Figures researched against the official sources listed at the end on 06/10/2026; key figures re-checked on gov.uk on 16/10/2026. Confidence: high. Re-check by 06/01/2027, or sooner after a Budget or Spring Statement.

This page is for UK founders who need the personal tax numbers for the 2026-27 tax year (06/04/2026 to 05/04/2027): sole traders working out what to set aside, and company owners deciding between salary and dividends. It covers Income Tax, the dividend and savings rules, and National Insurance for the self-employed, with a short note on employees and directors. It is reference, not advice. Figures are for England, Wales and Northern Ireland unless stated.

## Personal Allowance

- **£12,570** of income is tax free for most people.
- **Taper:** if your adjusted net income is over £100,000, the allowance falls by £1 for every £2 above £100,000. It is zero at £125,140 or more. Income between £100,000 and £125,140 is effectively taxed at 60% as a result.
- **Frozen:** Budget 2025 set the Personal Allowance at £12,570 and the basic rate limit at £37,700 up to and including 2030-31.

## Income Tax bands and rates (England, Wales, Northern Ireland)

| Band | Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Personal Allowance | Up to £12,570 | 0% |
| Basic rate | £12,571 to £50,270 | 20% |
| Higher rate | £50,271 to £125,140 | 40% |
| Additional rate | Over £125,140 | 45% |

**Scotland:** Scottish taxpayers pay different rates on earnings and trading profits, with seven bands for 2026-27 (starter, basic, intermediate, higher, advanced and top rates). See [Income Tax in Scotland](https://www.gov.uk/scottish-income-tax). Scottish taxpayers pay the same tax as the rest of the UK on dividends and savings interest, so the tables below apply UK-wide.

## Dividends (2026-27)

Dividends are how most limited company owners take profit. They are paid out of profit after Corporation Tax.

- **Dividend allowance:** £500 a year, taxed at 0%.
- Dividends sit on top of your other income to decide which band they fall in.

| Band | Dividend rate 2026-27 |
|---|---|
| Basic rate | 10.75% |
| Higher rate | 35.75% |
| Additional rate | 39.35% |

**What changed:** from 06/04/2026 the dividend ordinary rate rose to 10.75% and the upper rate to 35.75%, announced at Budget 2025. The additional rate stayed at 39.35%. Earlier tax years used lower basic and higher rates, so do not copy last year's calculation.

Example: a director with a £12,570 salary and £20,000 of dividends. The first £500 of dividends is at 0%. The remaining £19,500 falls in the basic rate band and is taxed at 10.75%, which is £2,096.25. For the full salary-versus-dividend picture, see [paying yourself as a director](paying-yourself-as-a-director.md).

## Savings interest (brief)

| Item | 2026-27 |
|---|---|
| Personal Savings Allowance, basic rate taxpayer | £1,000 |
| Personal Savings Allowance, higher rate taxpayer | £500 |
| Personal Savings Allowance, additional rate taxpayer | £0 |
| Starting rate for savings | Up to £5,000 at 0% |

The starting rate applies only if your other taxable income is under £17,570; each £1 of other income above the Personal Allowance reduces it by £1. Savings above the allowances are taxed at 20%, 40% or 45% in 2026-27.

**Coming next:** Budget 2025 confirmed savings and property income rates rise to 22%, 42% and 47% from 06/04/2027. Landlords and people with large cash balances should plan for that.

## National Insurance for the self-employed (2026-27)

Sole traders pay National Insurance on profits through Self Assessment, at the same time as Income Tax (31 January and 31 July). See [self assessment](self-assessment.md).

| Item | 2026-27 |
|---|---|
| Small Profits Threshold | £7,105 |
| Lower Profits Limit | £12,570 |
| Upper Profits Limit | £50,270 |
| Class 4 rate on profits £12,570 to £50,270 | 6% |
| Class 4 rate on profits over £50,270 | 2% |
| Class 2 voluntary rate | £3.65 a week |

**Class 2: current position.** Self-employed people no longer have to pay Class 2. If your profits are £7,105 or more, you are treated as having paid it, which protects your State Pension record at no cost. If profits are below £7,105, you can choose to pay voluntary Class 2 at £3.65 a week to keep building your National Insurance record.

**Class 4** is the main National Insurance cost for a sole trader.

Example: a sole trader with £40,000 profit in 2026-27 and no other income.

- Income Tax: (£40,000 minus £12,570) x 20% = £5,486
- Class 4: (£40,000 minus £12,570) x 6% = £1,645.80
- Total: £7,131.80, about 18% of profit.

## Class 1 for employees and directors (brief)

If your limited company pays you a salary, you are an employee for National Insurance. 2026-27 figures:

| Item | Annual | Monthly |
|---|---|---|
| Lower Earnings Limit | £6,708 | £559 |
| Primary Threshold (employee starts paying) | £12,570 | £1,048 |
| Secondary Threshold (employer starts paying) | £5,000 | £417 |
| Upper Earnings Limit | £50,270 | £4,189 |

- Employee rate: 8% between the Primary Threshold and the Upper Earnings Limit, 2% above.
- Employer rate: 15% above the Secondary Threshold.
- Employment Allowance: up to £10,500 off the employer's bill for eligible employers. GOV.UK: if a company has only one director, they must not be the only employee liable for secondary Class 1. So a one-person company cannot claim it. See [employing people](employing-people.md).
- Directors' National Insurance is worked out on total pay for the tax year so far (an annual basis), not pay period by pay period, though payroll can use a per-period method and reconcile at year end.

Detail and the usual salary choices are in [paying yourself as a director](paying-yourself-as-a-director.md).

## What to do now

1. Sole traders: set aside roughly the combined rate for your band (20% tax plus 6% Class 4 in the basic band) from every payment received.
2. Directors: recalculate your dividend plan with the 10.75% and 35.75% rates.
3. Watch the £100,000 taper: pension contributions can reduce adjusted net income.
4. Put payment dates in [key deadlines calendar](key-deadlines-calendar.md).
