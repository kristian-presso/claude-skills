# VAT registration, thresholds and rates

## The thresholds

| Threshold | Amount | In force since |
|---|---|---|
| Registration threshold (taxable turnover) | £90,000 | 01/04/2024 (was £85,000) |
| Deregistration threshold | £88,000 | 01/04/2024 (was £83,000) |

HMRC's VAT Notice 700/1 supplement lists £90,000 and £88,000 as current. The registration threshold does not apply to a business based outside the UK: a non-established business must register as soon as it makes any taxable supply in the UK.

## What counts as "taxable turnover"

Taxable turnover is everything you sell that is not VAT exempt or outside the scope of VAT. It includes zero-rated and reduced-rated sales as well as standard-rated ones. It also includes services you buy from businesses abroad that you have to "reverse charge" (see below). That catches founders out: a big spend on overseas software or contractors can push you over £90,000 even if your own sales are lower.

If you only sell exempt or out-of-scope things, you do not have to register.

## The two tests

You must register if either test is met.

**1. Rolling 12-month test (looking back).** At the end of every month, add up your taxable turnover for the last 12 months. It is a rolling window, not your tax year or financial year. If it goes over £90,000:

- register within 30 days of the end of the month you went over
- your effective date of registration is the first day of the second month after you went over

GOV.UK example: turnover passes the threshold on 15 July. You must register by 30 August, and you are VAT registered from 1 September.

**2. 30-day forward-look test.** If you expect taxable turnover in the next 30 days alone to go over £90,000, you must register by the end of that 30-day period. Your effective date is the date you realised, not the date turnover actually passed the threshold. GOV.UK example: you sign a £100,000 contract on 1 May. You must apply by 30 May and you are registered from 1 May.

If you go over only temporarily, you can apply by post (form VAT1) for a registration "exception". **Late registration:** you owe VAT on all sales from the date you should have registered, whether or not you charged it, and you may get a penalty.

## Voluntary registration

You can register below £90,000 if you are genuinely in business. You can register before you start trading, and you can ask for voluntary registration to be backdated by up to 4 years. Pick the date carefully: HMRC says you cannot change it later.

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Reclaim VAT on business costs and equipment | You must add VAT (usually 20%) to your prices |
| Reclaim VAT on purchases before registration: goods you still have, bought in the last 4 years; services from the last 6 months | Consumers cannot reclaim VAT, so you either raise prices or absorb the cost |
| Business customers can reclaim the VAT you charge, so it often costs them nothing | Making Tax Digital software, regular returns and penalties for late filing or payment |
| Can look more established to larger clients | You stay registered until you cancel, and a cancellation for low turnover cannot be backdated |

Rule of thumb: registering early tends to suit businesses that sell mainly to VAT-registered businesses or have high VATable costs. It tends to hurt businesses that sell mainly to the public.

## How to register

1. Register online through HMRC's "register for VAT" service. Have ready: your company number (companies) or National Insurance number and ID (individuals), your UTR, bank details, turnover so far and a 12-month estimate.
2. HMRC posts your 9-digit VAT number, your effective date of registration and your first return dates, and signs you up for Making Tax Digital for VAT.
3. Set up your VAT online account as soon as the number arrives.
4. If you have not heard after 40 working days, contact the VAT Registration Service. HMRC asks you not to chase sooner.

**While you wait:** you owe VAT from your effective date but cannot show VAT on invoices yet. Raise your prices by the VAT amount instead. Once you have your number, reissue those invoices showing the VAT, so business customers can reclaim it.

**Cancelling:** you must cancel within 30 days if you stop being eligible, for example if you stop trading. You may cancel if taxable turnover falls below £88,000. HMRC usually confirms within 40 working days. You cannot backdate a cancellation because turnover fell. You must keep VAT records for 6 years after cancelling.

## VAT rates

| Rate | % | Examples relevant to small businesses |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | 20% | Most services (consulting, design, coaching), software, apps and other digital downloads, audiobooks, hot takeaway food, confectionery, crisps, soft drinks, catering and restaurant meals |
| Reduced | 5% | Children's car seats, domestic fuel and power |
| Zero | 0% | Most food, books, newspapers and magazines, e-books and other e-publications, children's clothes and footwear, sanitary products |
| Exempt | No VAT | Financial services and insurance, education by an eligible body (not private schools), healthcare, most land and property |
| Outside the scope | Not in the VAT system | Statutory fees (such as the London congestion charge), donations given with nothing in return, goods or services bought and used outside the UK |

**Zero-rated is not exempt.** Zero-rated sales count towards the threshold and you can reclaim VAT on related costs. Exempt sales do not count, and you cannot reclaim VAT on costs used to make them.

**Digital products:** e-books, e-newspapers and e-magazines have been zero-rated since 01/05/2020. An e-publication that is more than half advertising, audio or video is standard-rated, as are audiobooks, apps and software.

**Temporary relief now ended:** from 25/06/2026 to 01/09/2026 a temporary reduced rate applied to some children's meals and to children's and family admissions to some attractions. Do not apply it to sales after it ended.

## Selling digital services to EU consumers

Since Brexit, UK businesses are outside the EU VAT system. The rules for "electronically supplied services" (automatic downloads, pre-recorded online courses, software, streaming) are:

- **UK consumers:** UK VAT, once you are registered.
- **EU consumers:** VAT is due in the customer's EU country at that country's rate. The EU's €10,000 small-seller threshold only applies to businesses established in the EU, so a UK business is caught from the first sale.
- **How to account:** either register in every EU country you sell to, or register once for the EU **non-Union One Stop Shop (OSS)** in any one EU country you choose. You file one quarterly OSS return, due by the end of the month after each quarter, and that country passes the VAT on.
- **Marketplaces:** if a platform or app store is responsible for the VAT, you do not account for it yourself.
- Live webinars, live tutoring and made-to-order digital content are not "electronically supplied", so different rules apply.

## Reverse charge on services bought from abroad

When a UK VAT-registered business buys most business-to-business services from an overseas supplier (for example US software, ads or freelancers), the supplier does not charge UK VAT. You account for it instead: add VAT on the full value as output tax (box 1), reclaim the same amount as input tax (box 4), and put the value in boxes 6 and 7. If you can fully reclaim VAT, the net cost is nil. If you are not yet registered, these purchases still count towards your £90,000 taxable turnover.

## What to do

1. Check rolling 12-month taxable turnover at every month end, including reverse-charge services.
2. Diarise the 30-day deadline the moment you go over, or sign a contract that will.
3. Decide on voluntary registration based on who your customers are.
4. Check the rate for each product line, especially food and digital goods.
5. Set up returns and software: see [vat schemes mtd and returns](vat-schemes-mtd-and-returns.md) and [key deadlines calendar](key-deadlines-calendar.md). Changes are logged in [changes ledger](changes-ledger.md).
