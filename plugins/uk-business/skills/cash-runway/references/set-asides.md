# Estimating what you owe HMRC

> Reference, not tax or accounting advice. Rates as of 16/10/2026, checked on gov.uk that day. Re-check after any Budget or at the start of each tax year (6 April) or financial year (1 April). If an accounting tool or an accountant gives you a figure, use theirs over these estimates.

The point of a set-aside is simple: money in the bank that already belongs to HMRC is not yours to spend. The three big ones for a small limited company are VAT, Corporation Tax and payroll taxes. Work out each one, then take them away from cash.

Always prefer an exact figure over an estimate, and an estimate over a blank. If you cannot estimate one, say so and treat it as unknown, never as zero.

## VAT owed

**Best source:** the VAT liability in the accounting software for the open period, plus any filed return not yet paid.

**Estimate if you only have the bank feed or invoices:**

| Scheme | Estimate for the period so far |
|---|---|
| Standard (invoice basis) | VAT on sales invoiced in the period minus VAT on purchases. If sales are all at 20%, VAT on sales is gross sales x 1/6 |
| Cash Accounting Scheme | Same sum, but on money received and paid in the period, not invoices |
| Flat Rate Scheme | Your flat rate % x VAT-inclusive turnover received or invoiced in the period. Limited cost traders use 16.5% |
| Annual Accounting Scheme | Estimated annual liability minus the interim payments already made |

Include any **filed but unpaid** return: it is due 1 month and 7 days after the period ends.

The VAT rates (standard 20%, reduced 5%, zero 0%) and the Flat Rate Scheme 16.5% limited cost trader rate are current as of 16/10/2026. Verify at gov.uk/vat-rates and gov.uk/vat-flat-rate-scheme.

## Corporation Tax set-aside

Two parts:

1. **Unpaid tax for finished periods.** Due 9 months and 1 day after each period ends. Take it from the CT600 or the accountant's computation.
2. **Accrued tax on this year's profit so far.** Estimate taxable profit to date (profit before tax, adding back entertaining and depreciation if you know them), then apply the rate.

Rates for the financial year starting 01/04/2026, unchanged since 01/04/2023 (verify at gov.uk/corporation-tax-rates):

| Annualised taxable profit (no associated companies) | Tax |
|---|---|
| £50,000 or less | 19% |
| £50,001 to £250,000 | 25% less Marginal Relief of 3/200 x (£250,000 minus profit). The extra pound in this band costs 26.5% |
| Over £250,000 | 25% |

Shortcut for a quick snapshot: set aside 19% of profit to date if the year looks like it will stay under £50,000, 25% if it will clearly go over £250,000, and work out Marginal Relief in between. The £50,000 and £250,000 limits shrink for short periods and are shared between associated companies.

## PAYE and National Insurance owed

**Best source:** the payroll software's "amount due to HMRC" for the current tax month, plus anything overdue.

**Estimate:** income tax and employee NI deducted from staff pay, plus employer NI, for every payroll run since the last payment to HMRC. Payments are due on the 22nd of the following tax month (tax months start on the 6th).

Employer NI for 2026-27 is 15% above the secondary threshold of £5,000 a year (£417 a month). Employee NI is 8% between £12,570 and £50,270 a year, 2% above. Employment Allowance (up to £10,500 off employer NI) is not available to a company whose only employee liable for employer NI is a sole director. Verify at gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027.

## Other commitments worth naming

These are not taxes, but they are cash you have already promised:

- dividends declared but not yet paid
- loan repayments and finance agreements due in the next month
- rent, payroll net pay and supplier bills already invoiced and due within 30 days, if the user wants a stricter view

Keep them on a separate line from the tax set-asides so the user can see which is which.

## Not included by default

- **The director's personal Self Assessment.** That is the director's money, not the company's. If the director pays it from the company account it becomes a director's loan, which has its own rules.
- **Future VAT, Corporation Tax and PAYE on next month's trading.** Runway already counts these as part of monthly outgoings, as long as HMRC payments appear in the bank history.
