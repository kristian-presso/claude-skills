# Why small venues lose bookings to slow email replies

By Dana Whitlock, events lead at a 120-capacity venue in Bristol. Published 2 October 2026.

Most venue enquiries are not lost on price. They are lost in the inbox.

Over the last twelve months we logged every enquiry that came in through our website form: 412 in total.
For each one we recorded how long it took us to send a first reply and whether it turned into a confirmed
booking.

## The one-hour line

Enquiries we answered within one hour converted at 38%. Enquiries answered the next day converted at 17%.
Nothing else we measured (price, day of week, event type) moved conversion anywhere near as much.

## Why speed matters so much

People planning an event email three to five venues at once. The first venue to reply with a clear answer
on availability and a rough price sets the anchor. Everyone after that is being compared with it.

## The four reply templates that cover most enquiries

We found that 80% of enquiries were one of four types: date availability, capacity and layout, catering
options, and price for a specific date. We wrote one template for each. Each template answers the question
in the first line, gives a price range, and offers a 15 minute call.

## What changed when we used them

Our median first-reply time dropped from 19 hours to 47 minutes in the first month. Confirmed bookings from
web enquiries rose from 41 in the six months before to 63 in the six months after.

## The objection we hear most

"We can't afford someone sitting on the inbox." You do not need to. The templates take two minutes each. We
set up a phone notification for the form and agreed that whoever is free replies.

## Why we stopped offering a downloadable brochure

We used to send a PDF brochure. Fewer than one in ten people opened it, according to our email tool. A short
reply with the answer and a price range did more.
