# Sequencing

When the hub and each derivative publish, and how to pace them.

## Hub first

The hub publishes before any derivative.

- Every derivative links to the hub, or to a page that links to it. If the hub is not live,
  that link is a 404.
- A derivative pointing at a missing page wastes the click and the reader's trust.
- Crawlers following the link find nothing, which does not help the hub get indexed.

In the map, the hub is sequence 1 at day 0. Derivatives are sequence 2 onwards, day 1 or
later.

## No orphans

Before each derivative goes out, check its link resolves to a live page. This includes
related pages, not just the hub. Treat it as a gate, not a nice-to-have.

## No repeated claims

No two rows share the same core claim. Two near-identical posts a day apart look like a
mistake. Merge them or change one angle.

## Default pacing

| Channel | Days after hub |
| --- | --- |
| Short social post | 1 |
| Email teaser | 3 to 5 |
| Short-form video | 5 to 7 |
| Carousel | 7 to 10 |
| Audio clip | 14 to 21 |
| Newsletter section | Next scheduled send |

These are starting points. Adjust for:

- **Audience overlap.** If the same people get the newsletter and follow the social account,
  leave more space.
- **Production time.** Video takes longer than a post. Set the lag to when it can really be
  ready.
- **Existing cadence.** Fit pieces into the channel's normal rhythm rather than adding
  extra sends.

## One piece per channel per week, per source

Two posts from the same article two days apart feel repetitive. Two posts from different
articles two days apart feel like a normal schedule. A large map may run six to eight weeks.
That is fine; the hub stays live throughout.

## Several sources at once

When more than one map is running, interleave by channel. Someone following one channel
should see varied sources, not six posts in a row from the same guide. This happens at the
channel calendar level, outside any single map.
