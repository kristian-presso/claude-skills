# Links and calls to action

## One link per derivative

Each derivative sends people to exactly one place.

- Derivatives are short. Two links split attention and both get fewer clicks.
- Each piece has one job: make one point and send the reader somewhere specific.

### Choosing the link

Default to the hub.

Link to a related page instead only when:

- The derivative's angle fits that page better than the whole hub, **and**
- That page itself links to the hub, so the reader is one click from it.

Never link one derivative to another (a social post pointing at an email, for example). It
creates paths that skip the hub.

### Anchor text

Where the channel shows link text, describe the destination: "our guide to dependency
auditing" rather than "click here". "Read the full guide" is fine as a CTA label.

### Platforms that suppress links

Some social platforms are widely believed to show posts with external links to fewer people.
Common workarounds are a link in the first comment or "link in bio". These are practitioner
observations, not documented platform rules, and they change. Follow what works on the
person's own account; check their past posts' performance if they have it.

## One call to action per derivative

Three asks at the end of a short piece usually produce none.

Good:

- "Read the full dependency-auditing guide."
- "Book a 20-minute walkthrough."
- "Reply with your setup and I will tell you which setting to change."

Weak:

- "Let me know what you think."
- "Read the guide, subscribe, and share this with your team."

### Placement

| Channel | Where the CTA goes |
| --- | --- |
| Social post | Last line |
| Thread | Last post |
| Email | After the body, before the sign-off |
| Short video | Spoken in the last few seconds, written in the caption |
| Carousel | Its own final slide |
| Newsletter section | End of the section |

### Keep the CTA and the link aligned

If the CTA says "read the full guide", the link is the hub. If it says "see the templates",
the link is the templates page. A mismatch confuses the reader and muddies the click data.

## Measuring it

Referrers are often stripped when people click from inside social apps, and UTM parameters
get lost in analytics tools that group by page path. The robust signal is the landing page
itself: if a derivative links to a specific page, a rise in visits to that page in the days
after it publishes is your measure. It is coarse, and at small volumes it is enough.
