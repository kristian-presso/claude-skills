# Channel formats

One claim per piece is the rule across every channel. The length and format norms below
are working defaults, last checked October 2026. Platforms change their limits; check the
platform's own help pages before building anything that depends on an exact number.

## The hub (canonical long-form)

The source. Not a derivative.

- Article, guide, report, documentation page, or a transcript turned into an article.
- Typically 1,000 to 4,000 words.
- Sequence 1, day 0. Everything links back here.
- May make several related claims, but has one thesis.

## Short social post (LinkedIn, X, Threads, Bluesky)

- **Format:** single post, or a short thread.
- **Length:** LinkedIn allows about 3,000 characters, but only the first two or three lines
  (roughly 200 characters) show before "see more", so the opening line carries the post.
  X allows 280 characters on a standard account. Threads allows 500. Bluesky allows 300.
- **How it differs from the hub:** one claim, stated in the first line, with the single
  best supporting detail. No context-setting paragraph.
- **Example:** "Most dependency audits run on a schedule. The ones that catch real problems
  run on every pull request. It is a three-line config change."

## Email teaser

- **Format:** plain or lightly styled, one section.
- **Length:** 100 to 250 words.
- **How it differs:** answers one sub-question the reader probably has, gives the useful
  part of the answer, and sends them to the hub for depth. It is not a summary of the hub.
- **Example subject:** "Why async check-ins fail in the first two weeks"

## Newsletter section

- **Format:** one item inside an existing newsletter.
- **Length:** 100 to 200 words.
- **How it differs:** one reason this specific audience should care, then the link.

## Short-form video (Reels, Shorts, TikTok, LinkedIn video)

- **Format:** talking head, screen recording, or simple animation.
- **Length:** 30 to 90 seconds works on every major platform.
- **How it differs:** shows one concrete example from the hub. One scene, not a summary.
- **CTA:** said in the last few seconds and written in the caption or description.

## Carousel or slide deck

- **Format:** one idea per slide.
- **Length:** 5 to 12 slides. Instagram allows up to 20 images per carousel; LinkedIn
  document posts take a PDF.
- **How it differs:** turns one section of the hub into a visual sequence. Title slide
  makes the claim, inner slides support it, last slide is the CTA.

## Audio clip or podcast segment

- **Format:** solo, interview, or Q and A.
- **Length:** 3 to 10 minutes standalone.
- **How it differs:** explores one question out loud, in conversation.

## Long-form derivative (guest post, second article)

- Only when a sub-topic of the hub deserves its own deep treatment for a different
  audience. Rewrite fully; never republish the hub word for word on another site without a
  canonical link back.

## Summary

| Channel | What makes it different from the hub |
| --- | --- |
| Short social post | One claim, first line, no preamble |
| Email teaser | One reader sub-question answered, link for depth |
| Newsletter section | One reason this audience should care |
| Short-form video | One concrete example shown |
| Carousel | One section visualised, one macro claim |
| Audio | One question explored in conversation |
