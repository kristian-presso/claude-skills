# Building a voice profile

Goal: a short, specific description of how this person writes, specific enough that a
draft can be checked against it line by line. "Professional but friendly" is useless. "Opens
with what they did, in under ten words; never asks a question at the end" is usable.

## What to collect

Three to five posts is the sweet spot. One is not enough to separate habit from accident.
More than eight adds little. Ask for posts the person likes, not just their most popular
ones: a viral post may not sound like them.

If they only have other writing (blog, newsletter, emails), use it, but note that LinkedIn
habits (length, line breaks) may differ.

## What to look for

Read all samples, then fill in each line. Quote short fragments as evidence in your own
notes, but never reuse them in drafts.

| Dimension | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Length | Typical word count? Do they vary it? |
| Openings | First line: a statement, a number, a question, a scene, a quote? How long? |
| Rhythm | One sentence per line, or paragraphs? How many line breaks? Sentence length? |
| Vocabulary | Plain or technical? Words or phrases they repeat? Words they clearly avoid? |
| Specifics | Do they use numbers, names, prices, places? Figures or words for numbers? |
| Tone | Warm, dry, blunt, playful, earnest? Do they use humour, and what kind? |
| Stance | Do they take sides? Hedge? Use "I" or "we"? Address "you"? |
| Endings | A question, a call to action, a one-line summary, or just stop? |
| Formatting | Emoji? Hashtags? Bullet lists? Bold? Dashes or no dashes? Exclamation marks? |
| Locale | British or American spelling? Currency and date style? |
| Topics and lines they would not cross | Things they never post about, claims they never make. |

## The profile template

```markdown
## Voice profile: <name>
Based on <N> posts, <date>.

- **Length:** <e.g. 80 to 150 words; rarely long>
- **Opening:** <e.g. a flat statement of what happened, under 10 words>
- **Rhythm:** <e.g. two-sentence paragraphs, a blank line between each>
- **Words:** <e.g. plain; says "customers" not "clients"; never "leverage", "game-changer">
- **Specifics:** <e.g. always a number; figures not words; names tools, not clients>
- **Tone:** <e.g. dry, a little impatient with jargon, no exclamation marks>
- **Endings:** <e.g. stops on the point; never a question>
- **Formatting:** <e.g. no emoji, no hashtags, occasional short bullet list>
- **Locale:** <e.g. British spelling, £, dates as 3 March>
- **Avoid:** <anything they clearly never do>
```

Keep it to ten lines or fewer. Show it to the person and adjust if they disagree; they know
their voice better than the samples do.

## Using the profile

- Draft against it, then read the draft next to two of the samples. If the draft would
  stand out in their feed as written by someone else, revise.
- Where the profile and the generic advice in this skill conflict, the profile wins. If
  they always end with a question, end with a question.
- Update the profile when they edit a draft in a consistent way ("you keep making it too
  formal"). Offer to save the updated version.

## Things that make drafts sound generic

Check for and remove unless the person genuinely writes this way:

- A one-word or two-word first line followed by a line break for drama.
- Every sentence on its own line.
- "Here's the thing", "Let that sink in", "Read that again".
- Lists of exactly three adjectives.
- A tidy moral at the end that restates the post.
- Rhetorical questions the post then answers.
