# Opening lines

On LinkedIn the first line or two is all most people see before "see more", roughly the
first 200 characters. The opening has one job: make the right reader want the second line.

The person's own voice profile overrides everything here. These are defaults for when their
samples do not settle it.

## Openings that tend to work

- **The specific result.** "We cut our quote turnaround from three days to four hours."
- **The plain statement of what happened.** "We stopped doing discovery calls for small
  orders."
- **A number that surprises.** "62% of our enquiries arrive after 6pm."
- **A named comparison.** "Our biggest competitor charges per seat. We charge per event."
- **The uncomfortable truth in their field.** "Most venue websites hide the price because
  the price is the problem."
- **A reversal of something they used to believe.** "Two years ago I told clients to post
  daily. I was wrong."

What these share: the point or the most interesting detail arrives in the first eight to
twelve words, and it is concrete.

## Openings to drop

These signal "template" and push readers on:

- "I've been thinking about..."
- "Here's something interesting..."
- "Let me tell you a story."
- "Hot take:" / "Unpopular opinion:"
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "It goes without saying..."
- "I'm humbled/thrilled/excited to announce..." (unless that is genuinely their style)
- A single dramatic word followed by a line break ("Failure.")
- Any opening that delays the point past the first sentence.

## Endings

- **Stop on the point.** Often the strongest ending is the last line of the argument.
- **One line they could repeat.** A short restatement in fresh words, not a summary.
- **One specific ask**, if it fits: "If you run a venue, reply with your average reply time
  and I will tell you where you stand."

Drop: "Thoughts?", "Agree?", "What do you think?", "Follow for more", and a closing tidy
moral that repeats the post.

## Words that read as filler

Cut unless the person genuinely uses them: leverage (as a verb), unlock, game-changer,
revolutionary, best-in-class, synergy, seamless, frictionless, delve, landscape, journey,
navigate (for non-physical things), empower.
